# Usability rounds: session script and prompts

**Draft v0.1, 16 Nov 2026**, for the Riphah ethics application (item 16 in `README.md`). Owner: Hamza Butt (V8). Not yet reviewed by the supervisor. This is the script the researchers follow in usability round 1 (22–26 Feb 2027) and round 2 (12–16 Apr 2027). Text in `[brackets]` is filled in before use. Nothing here is run before ethics approval.

**Who:** 5–8 students per round, each with Form G signed by a parent and their own assent. Two researchers (one leads and talks, one takes notes) and a teacher in the room. Wording of the opening script matches the student part of Form G in `CONSENT_FORMS.md`; if Form G changes, change this file with it.

**Aims** (`SRS.md` §14.3): find what is confusing, measure task success and SUS. Targets: SUS of at least 70 and at least 90% task success. In these small formative rounds the numbers are indicators only, and are reported either way.

## 1. Session plan (about 30 minutes)

| Min | Step | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Welcome, opening script, check assent | Lead |
| 3–5 | Set up the phone; remind: say what you think out loud | Lead |
| 5–22 | Tasks 1–8 with think-aloud (skip or shorten tasks if time runs out; note which) | Student; lead prompts; second researcher notes |
| 22–27 | SUS, 10 statements, read aloud if the student wants | Student; lead |
| 27–30 | Short debrief, thanks, answer questions | Lead |

Stop at any time if the student wants to, or looks upset (see §11). Offer a break between tasks if the student looks tired.

## 2. Before the session

- Phone charged, app installed, test class code and practice slips ready (test data only; never real student data). `[Device and build number]`.
- One note sheet per student (§7). Use a **session number** (S1, S2 …) in place of a name.
- Check Form G is signed and the student agrees before starting. If a student does not want to, thank them and stop. Nothing is recorded about the refusal.
- No audio, video or screen recording, and no photos. `[Unless the team adds a separate consent box to Form G, which needs ethics approval first.]`

## 3. Opening script (read aloud)

> Hello. My name is `[name]`. This is `[name]`, who will write notes, and this is `[teacher]`.
>
> We are testing the app, not you. If something is confusing, that is the app's fault, and it helps us to know. There are no right or wrong answers.
>
> I will ask you to do some things in the app. As you do them, please say what you are thinking, even small things like "I don't know what this means". I will not help you much, because I want to see what the app is like on its own. If you get stuck, that is useful for us.
>
> We are writing notes by hand. We are not recording your voice or the screen, and we are not writing down your name.
>
> You can stop at any time and it is fine to say no. Nothing bad happens, and your teacher will not be told how you did.
>
> Do you have any questions? Do you want to take part?

If the student says yes, tick "agreed" on the note sheet. If not, stop.

## 4. Tasks

Give each task in plain words. Do not use the app's own button names in the task. The "Stream library" task is given only if that screen exists in the build being tested.

| # | Task (what you say) | Success means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Here is a class code. Please join the class and choose a nickname." | Joins with the code and a nickname (not a real name) |
| 2 | "Your parent signed a paper form with a slip on it. Here is the slip. Show me what you would do with it." Also ask: "What if you did not have the slip?" | Enters the family slip code, or finds how to skip it, and understands what each choice means |
| 3 | "Please start the practice week and play it." | Starts and finishes the practice week |
| 4 | "Imagine you need a break. Please stop, then come back and carry on." | Pauses, leaves, returns, and resumes in the right place |
| 5 | "Please play week 1." | Finishes week 1 |
| 6 | "You are not sure what something means. Find help." | Opens the "?" help and finds an answer |
| 7 | "Show me your suggested paths. Pick one and tell me, in your own words, why the app suggested it." | Opens "Your paths", opens "Why this?", and explains one reason |
| 8 | "Is the result final? What would you do with it?" | Says it is a starting point, and mentions talking with family |
| 9 | *(only if built)* "Find out more about one study stream." | Finds the stream library and opens a stream page |

A task is **success** if finished without help, **partial** if finished after a hint, **fail** if not finished. Note the hint given. Give a hint only after about 1 minute of being stuck, and say "Would you like a hint?" first. Do not give hints for task 8: record what the student says.

## 5. Think-aloud prompts and probes

Use neutral words. Do not say "good", "right", "wrong" or "easy". Do not explain the app. Do not lead ("Did you find that confusing?").

**To keep talking (if quiet for about 10 seconds):**
- "What are you thinking now?"
- "Can you keep saying what you are looking at?"
- "What are you looking for?"

**Before an action:**
- "What do you expect will happen if you tap that?"
- "What do you think this button does?"

**After an action:**
- "Is that what you expected?"
- "What do you think just happened?"
- "What would you do next?"

**On wording and pictures:**
- "Can you tell me in your own words what this says?"
- "Is there any word here you are not sure about?"
- "What does this picture tell you?"
- "Did the read-aloud button help? When would you use it?" (ask only if they used or noticed it)

**On the results (tasks 7–8):**
- "What does this card tell you?"
- "How do you feel about this suggestion?" (note the answer; do not argue with it)
- "Who would you show this to? What would you say?"

**If the student asks "Am I doing it right?":** "There is no right or wrong. What would you do if I was not here?"

**Students from Urdu-medium schools:** English reading difficulty is part of what we are testing. Note each word or sentence that causes trouble. Do not translate it for them in task time. After the task you may ask what they thought it meant. Round 1 and 2 each aim to include at least 2 students from government or Urdu-medium schools (`SRS.md` §14.3).

## 6. What to note

For each task, the note-taker records:

- the result (success, partial, fail, skipped) and any hint given;
- errors (wrong taps, going to the wrong screen, going back);
- hesitations of about 5 seconds or more, and where;
- wording that confused them (quote the word or the screen, not the student's name);
- anything that looked worrying or upset the student, and anything they liked;
- time to finish, only if easy to note.

Write what happened, not your guess about why. Mark guesses as "(guess)".

## 8. System Usability Scale

Use the **standard 10-item System Usability Scale** (Brooke, 1996), with its **standard wording, unchanged**, in English. Do not copy the items into this repository or change them. The research team holds a printed copy of the scale. Students tick a box from "strongly disagree" to "strongly agree" for each statement (1–5).

- Read each statement aloud if the student wants. You may explain a single word only if the student asks, from a fixed word list `[agreed before round 1]`. Never explain the statement. Note any word asked about.
- Say before starting: "These statements are about the app. There are no right or wrong answers."
- **Scoring (standard method):** for odd-numbered statements, subtract 1 from the answer; for even-numbered statements, subtract the answer from 5; add the ten results and multiply by 2.5. The total is 0–100.
- Report the mean and range for the round, and the number of students. With 5–8 students, this is an indicator only.
- If a statement is left blank, do not guess it. Mark the sheet "incomplete" and leave that student out of the SUS mean.

## 9. Debrief (about 3 minutes)

Ask, one at a time, and write short answers:

1. "What was the best part?"
2. "What was the most confusing part?"
3. "Would you change anything?"
4. "Is there anything you would not want the app to know about you?"

Then:
- Tell the student the result is only a starting point, and thank them.
- Remind them they can ask for their data to be deleted, and say who to ask: `[Team contact]`.
- Do not tell them how they "scored" on SUS or tasks.
- Do not ask them to keep the session secret, or to tell classmates anything.

## 10. Note-taking rules

- **Written notes only.** No audio, no video, no screen recording, no photos.
- **No names.** Use the session number. Do not write the nickname the student chose, parents' names or anything that could identify them. If a student says their real name aloud, do not write it.
- Notes stay with the team, on paper in a locked place, or typed into a file on the team's encrypted drive on the same day, then the paper is shredded `[retention: set by the ethics approval]`.
- Keep Form G apart from the note sheets, so a name never sits next to a session number. `[Where signed Form G copies are kept: set by the ethics approval.]`
- Notes are not shared with teachers or parents. Findings are reported for the round as a whole, never for one student, and never for groups of fewer than 5.

## 11. If a student is upset or tells you something serious

Follow `PROTOCOL.md` §9. Stop the session, offer a break, and tell the student that stopping is fine. Do not carry on with that student that day. If the student tells you about harm or risk of harm: listen, do not promise secrecy, do not investigate, and pass it the same day to the school's safeguarding contact `[Name and phone, from Form D]`. Write down only what was said and who it was passed to, keep that note separate from the research notes, and give it to the supervisor.

## 12. After each round

1. Within 2 days, merge the note sheets into one list of problems. Mark each problem High, Medium or Low (stopped the student, slowed the student, or small).
2. Report per round: number of students, task success (successes ÷ tasks attempted), SUS mean and range, and the main problems and the fix chosen. Report this whether or not the targets are met.
3. Fix High problems before the next round, or write down why not.
