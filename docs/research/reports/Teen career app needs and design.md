# Short puzzle chapters, family reports, offline phones

Rahyaab should be **one app with a single light story world (a Pakistani town or bazaar journey) that holds about six to eight short mini-games in two families**. One to two **choice games** (a swipe deck and a branching story) measure RIASEC interests, and three to four **untimed casual puzzles** (pattern, spatial rotation, numbers, sequence or planning) measure aptitudes. Optional "day in the life" stream sims come after results. Each mini-game should take **2–5 minutes**. A sitting should last about **5–10 minutes**, and the full assessment about **25–35 minutes spread over three to five sittings**, so the first results arrive within a week. After that, use is episodic around Matric and Class 9/11 decisions, not daily. The genre choice follows from the evidence. Pakistani teens of both sexes share familiarity with **board/tile casual games (Ludo, Carrom) and casual puzzles**. Puzzle games have the best day-7 retention of any mobile genre. Median mobile sessions run about **4.5–6 minutes**. Battle-royale games skew male, are violent and use a lot of data. The stakeholder evidence makes "gameplay only" too thin for a *product*, though it is the right scope for the *assessment*. Pakistani students mostly receive **no guidance at all**. Parents make the decision. Teachers, not counsellors, will be the only adults on the school side. Every credible competitor wraps its assessment in results with reasons, a stream/career library, saving, sharing and a next step. The MVP should therefore be "games plus the minimum needed to act on them": an explained top-3 result with Plan B, a stream library with entry tests, an Urdu/English parent share, save/resume and retake. The design must also fit the phones and the law. Pakistan is **about 92% Android**, dominated by **4 GB budget phones**. **28% of women internet users use only someone else's phone.** The country led the world in **internet-shutdown losses in 2024**, and it still has **no enacted data-protection law**. That last fact makes the UK Children's Code and Google Play Families policy the practical rulebook. The biggest unknowns are what Pakistani students, parents and teachers themselves say they want, what they would pay, and whether game framing actually lowers anxiety for them. No published source answers these, so they need primary research.

## Families decide while schools offer almost no guidance

The documented student need in Pakistan is basic: help working out one's own interests and choosing a group, not a polished feature list. A random-sample survey of 380 secondary students in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa found **no trained career guides, no help with subject selection and no assessment tools for identifying students' interests** ([JDSS](https://ojs.jdss.org.pk/journal/article/view/1435)). A 2024 survey of 99 KP teachers rated secondary school the most important stage for career development. The same teachers reported no training and few materials ([SPARK](https://journal.suit.edu.pk/index.php/spark/article/view/1060)). In a Karachi study, **only 36.6% of high-school students had any school career counselling and 82.6% relied on parents' advice** ([IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/1435)). The same pattern holds afterwards: **70% of Quaid-i-Azam University students had never received career counselling** ([PIDE thesis](https://thesis.pide.org.pk/thesis/availability-of-career-counseling-in-pakistan-a-case-study-of-quaid-e-azam-university-islamabad/)). The stakes are high because switching an FSc group after starting costs two years ([Parhlai](https://parhlai.com/blog/pre-medical-vs-pre-engineering)). The rules are also shifting. In December 2025 the IBCC let matric arts students enter any intermediate group, including pre-medical and pre-engineering, starting from the next session ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/09/new-admission-policy-announced-for-arts-students-across-pakistan/amp/)). Punjab has added new matric groups in health sciences, agriculture, IT and entrepreneurship ([Geo News](https://www.geo.tv/latest/586495-punjab-introduces-new-groups-for-matric-students)). More open choices make good information more valuable. Narrow career awareness compounds the problem. An Indian survey of about 10,000 students reportedly found most aware of only about seven careers, though the original report was not located ([uLektz](https://news.ulektz.com/?p=31819)). This argues for a library that widens options beyond MBBS and engineering. Competition for medical seats sharpens the need for a Plan B: **about 140,000 candidates registered for MDCAT 2025 for roughly 22,000 seats** ([PMDC](https://pmdc.pk/Documents/press/Press%20release%20MDCAT%202025.pdf)).

Students' complaints about existing tools are thinly documented, but the pattern points one way. Vendors themselves concede that tests help the already-decided most. They also concede that scenario questions fail when students cannot relate to them ([CareerExplorer](https://www.careerexplorer.com/blog/why-career-tests-dont-work)). Indian practitioners warn that many tools are "personality quizzes rebranded as career assessments" with no published reliability ([Dheya](https://www.dheya.com/insights/career-assessment-accuracy-india)). Independent US student reviews criticise mismatched recommendations, binary questions, clunky interfaces and no action plan after the assessment. They value retakes and career browsing ([Panther's Tale](https://pantherstale.com/106133/news/how-xello-is-really-viewed); [Saratoga Falcon](https://saratogafalcon.org/?p=8989); [futuremeanswered](https://www.futuremeanswered.com/youscience-review); [Amador Valley Today](https://www.amadorvalleytoday.org/14684/news/counselors-roll-out-the-new-xello-program)). That translates into four student-facing needs: **Pakistani scenarios they recognise, reasons shown for every recommendation, an "exploring/unsure" path, and always a next step**. Students also revisit choices non-linearly. A three-year Swiss study found career planning ongoing and recursive ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/15378908)). A field experiment found a personalised results display increased information-seeking ([IZA](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp18490/1/citations/bibtex)). Both support saved items, history and retakes. On AI, teens are curious but uneasy. Half of Scottish 13–18-year-olds had rethought career goals because of AI ([STV](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/half-of-scottish-teenagers-say-ai-has-made-them-rethink-career-goals-survey-finds)), and over a third of UK teens fear AI will harm their job prospects ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/bbc-bitesize-expands-ai-careers-guide-for-teenagers)). A short "how careers are changing" note per stream answers a real worry. Pakistani teens' trust in algorithmic advice is unmeasured.

Parents are the decisive second user, and the evidence is stronger here. Among 200 Pakistani secondary students, **parental influence outweighed media**, and family, media and stereotype factors together explained 69% of variance in career choice ([Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9274975/)). Career decisions among Peshawar medical students were **collective family deliberations**, with marriage expectations and izzat norms constraining women ([Frontiers in Medicine 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2026.1755128/full)). Women gravitate to roles seen as "culturally acceptable and safe" ([arXiv 2508.16664](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16664)). Parents worry about co-educational campuses far from home ([UCD](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/entities/publication/740309be-520b-4072-b15b-cb665cdacae8)). The evidence is not one-sided, though. In a Karachi pilot, **71.6% of women doctors said medicine was their own choice** ([JPMA](https://archive.jpma.org.pk/article-details/8567)). The app should therefore show girls the full range without presuming restrictions. It should also answer the questions families actually ask: prospects, cost and duration of study, entry tests, and whether the course is available locally or at a women's college. No product found offers a parent-tailored explanation report. Xello's parent view is read-only and school-gated, Kuder links parents through a code from the student's account, and Mindler involves parents only in paid counselling ([Xello Help](https://help.xello.world/en-us/content/Get-Started/Xello-Families/GS_Families.htm); [Kuder](https://www.kuder.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/0846_Waukee-Navigator-Parent-Flier-1.pdf); [Mindler pricing](https://www.mindler.com/pricing)). That gap is Rahyaab's clearest differentiator. WhatsApp as the delivery channel is plausible but supported only by Gulf evidence ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/news/education/whatsapp-most-preferred-tool-for-uae-parents-survey)). Price anchors are sparse. Eduvision charges **PKR 4,000 per counselling session** ([Eduvision](https://www.eduvision.edu.pk/careers/index.php)), CareerCoachs **PKR 1,700** for a detailed report plus a 30-minute session ([CareerCoachs](https://careercoachs.com/online-career-counselling/pakistan)), and Maqsad about **Rs 500–600 a month** for test prep ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2023/03/16/despite-global-downturn-ed-tech-sector-sees-hope-with-maqsads-2-8-million-round)). No Pakistani willingness-to-pay data exists.

On the school side, the "counsellor" will in practice be a class teacher with little time. A Karachi study found "no concept" of career counsellors in Pakistani schools ([IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/1435)). About **30% of government schools have one teacher managing several classrooms** ([ProPakistani 2026](https://propakistani.pk/2026/06/24/30-of-govt-schools-in-pakistan-have-one-teacher-managing-multiple-classrooms/)). Global platforms converge on a small educator toolkit. Xello offers completion and engagement reports, alerts and exports ([Xello](https://xello.world/en/educator/)). YouScience added a one-page "Counselor Summary Report" in 2025 ([YouScience](https://www.youscience.com/resources/blog/whats-new-in-brightpath-back-to-school-2025-product-updates)). Indian Punjab is training **5,000 government teachers as "career mentors"** rather than hiring counsellors ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/punjab-partners-with-iit-madras-to-train-5000-teachers-as-career-mentors-1503522099.html)). Evaluations show digital tools work best as *preparation* for human guidance ([North East CA evaluation](https://ambition.northeast-ca.gov.uk/downloads/2003)). Teacher tasks must therefore take minutes per class: a roster with completion status, the class's stream distribution, flags (not started, interest–aptitude mismatch, rushed play), a printable one-page summary for parent-teacher meetings, and CSV export. Schools' hard constraint is infrastructure. Gallup Pakistan reports **internet in 7% of government vs 32% of private primary schools, and computer labs in 3% vs 17%** ([ProPakistani 2026](https://propakistani.pk/2026/06/24/30-of-govt-schools-in-pakistan-have-one-teacher-managing-multiple-classrooms/)). The product must therefore run on students' or families' phones, offline. Pakistani edtech has mostly sold business-to-business to schools ([ProPakistani 2021](https://propakistani.pk/2021/09/17/business-models-of-edtech-companies-in-pakistan/)). Beaconhouse buys through system deals and single-campus pilots ([Knowledge Platform](https://knowledgeplatform.com/beaconhouse-and-knowledge-platform-sign-education-technology-deal-for-100000-students/); [Tutopiya](https://www.tutopiya.com/blog/ai-buddy/beaconhouse-cantt-campus-lahore-ai-buddy-case-study/)). Public routes run through PEF partners, NIETE/Islamabad model schools and NAVTTC/TEVTA ([TF](https://www.tf.edu.pk/?p=1506); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/amp/40151461); [TVET Reform](https://tvetreform.org.pk/career-counselling-and-job-placement/)). Each of these will expect a data-processing agreement, consent workflow, Urdu, offline use and impact evidence. A technical/DAE pathway in the recommendations would make Rahyaab relevant to TEVTA.

## Pakistani teens play Ludo and puzzles; Rahyaab should look like them

No representative survey of Pakistani 14–18-year-olds' game preferences exists, but the weaker sources agree on a core set. Blog roundups name **PUBG Mobile, Free Fire, Ludo, Carrom and Subway Surfers**, and describe Free Fire as popular with students for quick play and low data use ([Pak Fun Games](https://pakfungames.blog/most-popular-mobile-games-in-pakistan-2025/); [Arena Lobby](https://www.arenalobby.pk/top-mobile-games-pakistani-gamers-love-in-2026/)). An Appfigures snapshot put **Yalla Ludo at #1 and Ludo King at #3** on Google Play Pakistan ([Appfigures](https://app.appfigures.com/top-apps/google-play/pakistan/games)). Globally in October 2025, **Block Blast!, Roblox and Free Fire** led downloads ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-october-2025)). Pakistani play-time studies use small, mostly male samples. In one Lahore sample, 46.8% played PUBG one to two hours a day and 9.7% more than five hours ([Khan et al. 2021](https://www.asianonlinejournals.com/index.php/AJSSMS/article/download/1815/1543)). Pakistani focus groups mention Ludo and PUBG with "considerable gender and age-related differences" ([PAFMJ](https://www.pafmj.org/PAFMJ/article/view/12207)). Players report escapism and social play as motives, alongside gendered toxicity ([NUST dissertation](https://nust.academia.edu/KomalSalman)). The nearest data on girls is from India, where women's top games were Candy Crush (39%) and word puzzles (22%) and 94% preferred free casual games ([CMR via Beebom](https://beebom.com/women-prefer-candy-crush-pubg-mobile-report/amp/)).

The best global teen data is US. **97% of boys and about 75% of girls play**. Only **17% of girls identify as gamers**, versus 62% of boys, and daily play is 61% for boys versus 22% for girls. Smartphones are the common gaming device for both sexes (70%). **Fun is the major reason to play for 87%**, while learning is major for only 13%. Competing matters to 36% of boys but 15% of girls ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/05/09/teens-and-video-games-today/)). Quantic Foundry finds women score higher on Design (customisation), Fantasy, Story and Completion, and men on Competition. Age predicts more than gender, and gender gaps shrink in Chinese samples, which suggests they are cultural ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2015/08/28/gender-differences-in-gaming/)).

Taken together, the case against a "real" game genre is strong. **Battle royale is male-skewed, violent, long-session and data-heavy.** Competitive formats appeal mostly to boys and add test-like pressure. Many girls do not see themselves as gamers. The genre that is familiar to both sexes and acceptable to parents is **casual board/tile and puzzle play**: bright, tactile, tap-based and short. A thin story layer, avatar customisation and collectible progress add the Story, Design and Completion motives that appeal across genders. The framing should lead with fun and self-discovery, not learning. A useful line is "activities/challenges", not "gaming", which keeps girls and parents on side.

Session evidence fits short mini-games. GameAnalytics benchmarks put the **global median mobile session at 4.45 minutes, with 4–5 sessions a day**. Puzzle games show the **highest day-7 retention of any genre**, stable across regions ([GameDev Reports, Q1 2024](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-benchmarks-in-mobile)). Median-tier sessions run about 5–6 minutes and top-tier 8–9 minutes ([GameDev Reports, 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)). These are all-age benchmarks; no teen-specific session data was found. Educational field data adds a warning about length. Kazakhstan's game for Grade 11 students achieved **92% tutorial completion, motivation of 5.6/7, usability (SUS) of 76 and low anxiety of 1.9/5** ([Tokzhigitova et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1643246/full)). The team's own design notes record that completion in that trial fell from 57% to 41% across levels. That points to short chapters and early results.

Age-appropriateness is mostly about content and mechanics, not art style. Two requirements are well grounded: no violence, and no open chat or public leaderboards, given that **43% of US teen players report harassment** ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/05/09/teens-and-video-games-today/)). A third is a precaution: pymetrics-style money-splitting and card-deck tasks could read as gambling to Pakistani parents, so reskin or drop them. Rating guidance is mostly unconfirmed. PEGI/IARC rules were not fetched, so an "Everyone"-level rating is a sensible target but not a verified requirement. What teens find "childish vs cool" has no empirical study. NN/g's usability research is the closest guide: teens reject anything labelled for kids or written condescendingly ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/)). So the visual tone should be young-adult casual, Ludo-and-Block-Blast clean, not a cartoon mascot.

## Competitors split into lab-task batteries and story worlds

Two families of assessment game exist, and Rahyaab should borrow from both. **Lab-task batteries** come from hiring. Pymetrics runs **12 games of about 2–3 minutes each, roughly 25 minutes in total**: balloon risk, Tower of Hanoi, money exchange, keypress, easy/hard choice, digit memory, stop-signal, arrows, lengths, cards and faces ([MyConsultingCoach](https://www.myconsultingcoach.com/bcg-pymetrics)). Arctic Shores uses up to nine tasks in 20–45 minutes with an unscored practice task that also checks the device ([Psychometric Success](https://psychometric-success.com/aptitude-tests/test-types/arctic-shores-test)). HireVue uses four cognitive games ([Leutner et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.942662/full)). The only education product in this family is YouScience. It runs timed aptitude "brain games", including number-pattern prediction (about 15 minutes), 2-D/3-D spatial visualisation (about 7 minutes) and idea generation (about 5 minutes). Results carry labels such as "3D Visualizer" ([Cobb County handout](https://media.cobbk12.org/media/WWWCobb/fgg/560/Juniors%20-%20YouScience%20Information-1.pdf); [YouScience](https://www.youscience.com/idea-generation/)). Brightpath totals **about 60–90 minutes across 11 games, splittable over sittings** ([JEL](https://www.jelcc.com/careerdiscovery)). **Story worlds** include Knack's Wasabi Waiter, a sushi-service game ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2013/12/01/246999632/playing-the-game-to-get-the-job)), Kazakhstan's 3-D exploration, Brazil's six RIASEC islands ([Univali](https://periodicos.univali.br/index.php/acotb/article/view/22308)) and Ecuador's RIASEC narrative. In Ecuador, in-game scores correlated **above .75 with the traditional test** for all six types. Narrative quality (β = .40) and "vocational impact" (β = .38) predicted satisfaction, though the paper's venue is unconfirmed ([UA JDI 2026](https://web.ua.es/va/phdinf/documentos/jdi-2026/rommeljairgutierrezyepez.pdf)). Student-facing products such as Xello and Mindler use gamified questionnaires, not true games ([Xello Help](https://help.xello.world/en-us/Content/Knowledge-Base/Xello-6-12/Assessments/Students-Matchmaker.htm)).

The measurement literature explains why the hybrid matters. In a gamified situational-judgement test, story "missions" raised convergence with established scales to **β = .45–.57, versus .17–.39 for plain text** ([Georgiou et al. 2019](https://skillup.coreconsulting.it/home/pdfs/Georgiou,%20K.,%20Gouras,%20A.,%20&%20Nikolaou,%20I.%20(2019).%20Gamification%20in%20employee%20selection%20The%20developme.pdf)). Puzzles capture aptitude well, with game-score reliabilities of .77–.92 in adults ([Quiroga et al. 2019](https://zigzaghr.be/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Quiroga_emergent-games_intelligence.pdf)). Short forms are weak, though: 12 matrix items reached only .65–.70 ([Shute, Ventura & Ke 2015](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/portal1.pdf)). Each aptitude therefore needs roughly 20–30 calibrated items, or adaptive selection, before it can drive a recommendation. This sits uneasily with the team's plan of five puzzles in about six minutes. Three to four aptitudes at three to four minutes each, adaptively selected, is a better trade. Pymetrics' trait games (risk, fairness, inhibition) measure hiring constructs with no shown link to stream fit. The team's design already keeps its BART, Emotify and Easy/Hard clones out of scoring, and the evidence supports that.

The surrounding product shows what "table stakes" means. Every mature product pairs its assessment with **results, a career library and saved items**. US school platforms add goals, course planners, portfolios and parent views ([Xello features](https://xello.world/en/features-gallery/); [Kuder](https://help.kuder.com/en/articles/8173342-get-to-know-navigator)). Indian and Pakistani players run a **free teaser → paid report → paid counsellor** funnel: Mindler charges ₹2,400 for a 25-page stream report and ₹11,400 with counselling ([Mindler pricing](https://www.mindler.com/pricing)). Assessment length ranges from **10–20 minutes for O\*NET's profiler** ([O\*NET](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html)) to **about 2.5 hours for Mindler**. Results pages use five patterns: an archetype label, trait percentages, top-N lists (Mindler's top 2 streams, Naviance's top 3 of 10 themes), fit scores, and long PDFs. Mindler's counselling ends in a "best fit plus a backup" shortlist, which maps directly onto Pre-Medical versus Pre-Engineering/ICS. No product uses streaks, XP or badges outside the assessment itself, and no vendor publishes retention data. US platforms' engagement comes from teacher-assigned lessons, not organic use ([Xello Help](https://help.xello.world/en-CA/Content/Knowledge-Base/KB/Report_Student-Engagement.htm)). A Pakistani consumer app should expect **episodic use around decision points**, not daily habits. The competitor journey to copy runs: light onboarding → assessment in short sittings → top stream plus backup with reasons → explore careers under each stream → save → share with parents → optional counsellor and a retake prompt before the next decision.

## Design rules converge on calm, legible, untimed and honest

Teen usability research sets the baseline. NN/g studied 100 teens aged 13–17 and found they complete fewer tasks than adults. The causes were weaker reading, weaker search strategies and less patience. Teens skip dense text, leave slow pages, and did worst on school and government sites. Interactive quizzes and games work well with them, and NN/g advises writing at a **6th-grade reading level or lower**, designing for touch rather than drag precision, and avoiding forced registration ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/)). The risk for Rahyaab is therefore its information content, not its games: results and stream pages written like government notices. Pakistani literacy data makes this sharper. In ASER 2023 (rural), only **49% of Grade 5 children could read a Grade 2 Urdu story and 29% could read English sentences** ([State of Children](https://stateofchildren.com/annual-status-of-education-pakistan-2023-provisional)). The results are flat or falling since 2019. That makes Urdu-first copy, one instruction per screen, icons and optional Urdu audio usability requirements, not extras.

The accessibility numbers are standard. They include touch targets of **48×48 dp** on Android ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)) and WCAG 2.2's 24×24 px minimum ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Text contrast must be **4.5:1**, and colour must never be the only cue: green-valid/red-invalid without an icon fails ([W3C 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)). Colour-vision deficiency is common enough to matter. Pakistani studies find **1.4–5.8% of males** affected, and many do not know it ([Pak J Zool](https://zsp.com.pk/pdf48/1045-1049%20%2820%29%20QPJZ-0391-2015%2019-4-16%20%28revised%20and%20highlighted%20%29%20%282%29%20%281%29.pdf); [PAFMJ](https://www.pafmj.org/PAFMJ/article/download/2289/1985)). That is roughly one boy in twenty in a class. Nielsen's heuristics translate directly: show "Game 2 of 6", allow pause and exit with autosave, confirm before abandoning a game, keep a "?" for instructions mid-game, and use local terms such as Matric, FSc and ICS ([NN/g heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/)).

Colour should be chosen for contrast, colour-blind safety and cultural fit, not "psychology". The famous claim that red impairs test performance failed replication ([von Stumm 2015](https://eprints-gro.gold.ac.uk/11188/1/PSY-vonStumm2015.pdf)). A 2020 meta-analysis found **no evidential value** after correcting for publication bias ([Psychonomic Bulletin & Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-020-01772-1)). "Blue calms test-takers" belongs in the same bin. Green is positive and tied to national identity in Pakistan ([WorldAtlas](https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/what-do-the-colors-and-symbols-of-the-flag-of-pakistan-mean.html)). A teal-shifted green primary is a safe choice; exact flag green-and-white risks reading as official. Build tonal palettes with Material's HCT system, where tones about 50 apart give roughly 4.5:1 contrast ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/styles/color/system)). Interest items have no right answers, so selections get a neutral brand highlight plus a check icon. Red is reserved for real system errors, always with an icon and text. The six RIASEC types need a colour-blind-tested palette that also differs by lightness and always carries labels.

Urdu typography is a real engineering constraint. Urdu readers expect **Nastaliq**, and Naskh reads as "Arabic" ([UGA Linguistics](https://www.linguistics.uga.edu/news/stories/2023/linguistics-undergraduate-student-zahin-hoque-wins-fall-2023-curo-research-award)). No controlled study compares their mobile readability. Nastaliq needs far more vertical space. Practitioners recommend **a body line-height of about 1.8**, because 1.5 clips the dots above and below letters ([Open Design](https://open-design.ai/plugins/design-system-urdu/)). Fixed-height containers clip glyphs ([Chromium](https://codereview.chromium.org/1386343002/)), and Noto Nastaliq Urdu needs Android padding fixes ([ProAndroidDev](https://proandroiddev.com/taming-noto-nastaliq-fixing-urdu-font-rendering-issues-in-android-c65e83b40b34)). The practical rules are these:

- Use Noto Nastaliq Urdu or Gulzar. Jameel Noori's embedding licence is unverified.
- Set Urdu text about 2–4 sp larger than the English equivalent.
- Let containers grow with their text (`wrap_content`); never fix heights.
- Lay out with start/end, not left/right, and mirror navigation in Urdu.
- Do not mirror charts or media controls.
- Bidi-isolate English terms and numbers inside Urdu strings.
- Test at 130–200% font scale.

The assessment should feel like play, not an exam. The evidence is suggestive rather than proven. Untimed conditions tend to help anxious students ([PMC2806671](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2806671)). Gamification has a small positive effect on intrinsic motivation, autonomy and relatedness, with minimal effect on competence ([ETR&D meta-analysis 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)). Results on test anxiety are mixed, including a null result in 251 medical students ([MetaTOC](https://metatoc.com/papers/97555-game-based-assessment-investigating-the-impact-on-test-anxiety-and-exam-performance)). Rahyaab should claim engagement, not reduced anxiety, and measure anxiety in its pilot. The design rules follow. Show **no visible countdowns**, record time silently and never score on it. Avoid "test", "exam" and "score" in copy (in Urdu, use activity or game words rather than امتحان and ٹیسٹ, subject to testing). Let students choose the order of games. Give informational feedback ("you prefer hands-on puzzles"), not ranks, and never use leaderboards.

Ethical gamification has a clear external benchmark. The UK Children's Code treats sticky features (reward loops, notifications, autoplay) that make disengaging hard as likely unfair. It asks for neutral continue prompts, an active choice to keep going, and pausing without loss ([ICO Standard 5](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/)). It also forbids nudging children to share data or to overstate their age ([ICO Standard 13](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-data-protection/ico-codes-of-practice/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques)). Streaks work: they significantly raised Peruvian pupils' use of a maths platform, plausibly through loss aversion ([Northwestern IPR 2026](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf)). That effectiveness is exactly why they are ethically risky. Rewards contingent on performance can also undermine intrinsic motivation ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan, via ETR&D](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)). Google Play Families policy restricts ads SDKs, device identifiers and precise location for apps aimed at minors ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en)). The resulting rule set:

- Rewards for *finishing*, never for particular answers.
- Badges that never decay.
- No streaks, loot boxes or confirmshaming ("Quit and lose everything?").
- Neutral exit copy ("Saved. Come back anytime.").
- Opt-in reminders only, at most about weekly, and never at night. This is a practice figure; no authoritative number exists.
- No ads.

Results visualisation should favour accuracy over the RIASEC hexagon's charm. A radar chart's area changes with axis order: one example shrank from 19% to 2% of the chart ([PerThirtySix](https://perthirtysix.com/newsletters/17)). People also judge radial lengths less accurately than aligned bars ([Observable](https://observablehq.com/blog/avoid-radar-charts)). The primary view should be **top-3 strength cards and sorted horizontal bars** in qualitative bands ("strong / moderate / emerging"). The hexagon can be an optional shareable "shape" with fixed axis order. Results should state that they reflect today's play and can change.

## Budget Androids, shutdowns and an unpassed law set the floor

The target device is a 4 GB budget Android. Android holds **about 92% of Pakistan's mobile web traffic**. Android 13 is the largest version at about 18%, and Android 11–16 are all significant ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile-tablet/pakistan); [StatCounter versions](https://gs.statcounter.com/android-version-market-share/mobile-tablet/pakistan)). Transsion brands (Infinix, Tecno, Itel) held a reported **44% of units in Q2 2025**, ahead of Xiaomi (18%), Vivo (17%) and Samsung (10%) ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/10/20/xiaomi-eyes-pakistans-expanding-smartphone-market-as-local-assembly-and-smart-tv-production-gain-pace/)). Current entry models such as the Tecno Spark 40C ship with **4 GB RAM and 128 GB storage** for about Rs 26,000 ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/price/tecno-spark-40c/)). Sharing is the norm for many girls. GSMA's 2026 report puts phone ownership at **68% of women versus 93% of men**, the widest gap surveyed, and finds **28% of women internet users access it only on someone else's phone (men 4%)** ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2649428/pakistan); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2012291)). Permission from male relatives is a known barrier to ownership ([Gallup Pakistan](https://galluppakistandigitalanalytics.com/?p=6753)). These are adult figures; no teen data exists. Shared-device design therefore matters: PIN-locked profiles, quick sign-out, no results in lock-screen notifications, and no need for a personal phone number when a school code will do.

Connectivity is fast enough but unreliable. The median mobile download is **24 Mbps** ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan)), and 4G averages 18.9 Mbps ([Digital Rights Monitor](https://digitalrightsmonitor.pk/mobile-internet-faster-than-wifi-in-pakistan/)). Data costs only **about Rs 27–29 per GB** ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/pakistan-ranks-6th-globally-for-cheapest-mobile-data/); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/08/13/telcos-could-increase-data-charges-for-survival/)). But Pakistan had the **world's highest shutdown cost in 2024: US$1.62 billion from 18 shutdowns lasting 9,735 hours** ([Digital Rights Foundation](https://digitalrightsfoundation.pk/internet-shutdowns-cost-pakistan-us1-62-billion-report/)). Regional data suspensions continued in 2025–26 ([Aaj News](https://english.aaj.tv/news/330432051/balochistan-internet-shutdown-mobile-data-suspended-today-and-on-sept-6-pakistan-news)). More than **2,000 of 12,665 electricity feeders face over 10 hours of daily load-shedding** ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1398879-more-than-2-000-feeders-face-over-10-hours-of-daily-loadshedding-na-told)). Data cost is therefore not the binding constraint. Free storage on media-crammed phones, interrupted downloads, outages and battery are. All games must work fully offline once downloaded. Every trial should be written to local storage at once and synced idempotently. A preliminary on-device result should appear even when the server is unreachable. The team's server-side Python scoring engine stays authoritative for the confirmed result.

Quality standards supply the numeric thresholds. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 provides the NFR taxonomy, with new Safety and "Interaction capability" characteristics ([arc42](https://quality.arc42.org/articles/iso-25010-update-2023)). Google Play has required **targetSdk 36 for new apps and updates since 31 August 2026** ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)). Android vitals flags apps above a **1.09% user-perceived crash rate or 0.47% ANR rate**, or 8% on any device model ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/vitals)). It flags startup as excessive at **5 s cold and 2 s warm** ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9844486?hl=en-GB)). OWASP MASVS v2.1 adds a privacy control group for mobile apps ([OWASP MAS](https://mas.owasp.org/news/2024/01/18/masvs-v210-release--masvs-privacy/)).

The legal floor is a gap, so self-regulation must fill it. Pakistan's Personal Data Protection Bill was still **not enacted as of 2026**, and its regulator is not operating ([Chambers 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/pakistan)). The draft requires parent or guardian consent for children and states that **"tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited"** ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/pakistan); [MoITT draft](https://moitt.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/Final%20Draft%20Personal%20Data%20Protection%20Bill%20May%202023.pdf)). Proposed fines reach about USD 2 million ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2024/05/22/it-ministry-finalizes-data-protection-bill-with-fines-up-to-2-million/)). That clause matters directly for a game that scores telemetry. If the bill passes as drafted, gameplay logging could be read as behavioural monitoring of children. Rahyaab should keep logging purpose-limited to scoring and research, explain it to parents in plain Urdu, and collect verifiable consent now. Public-sector deployment adds data residency. The Cloud First Policy classifies government data into tiers, and the May 2026 accreditation drive pushes sensitive data onto servers inside Pakistan ([Profit](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/05/26/pakistan-launches-cloud-provider-accreditation-under-new-data-localization-push)). No hyperscaler region exists in Pakistan ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/technology-ai-research-insights/blogs/hyperscalers-continue-global-data-center-expansion-despite-geopolitical-tensions)). A UAE or Mumbai host is lawful for a private pilot today. The containerised FastAPI/PostgreSQL stack should stay portable to an accredited Pakistani cloud.

## Answers to the design questions

**Genre.** Rahyaab should use **casual puzzle plus board/tile mechanics inside a light narrative world**. The interest games are **choice games**: a swipe "bazaar" deck and a branching town story, in the "interactive narrative / card-choice" casual genre. The aptitude games are **casual logic puzzles**: matrix tiles, shape rotation, shop arithmetic, symbol sequences and grid planning. The look borrows from Ludo, Carrom and Block Blast: bright, flat and tactile. This is the one genre family Pakistani girls and boys both know, that parents accept, that has the strongest retention, and that runs well on low-end phones. Action, battle-royale, racing, competitive multiplayer and casino/card mechanics are out. Speed-scored reaction tasks are out of scoring because touch latency varies across cheap phones.

**Single versus multiple games.** Rahyaab should be **one game world with multiple mini-games**, not one monolithic game and not a menu of unrelated games. A single world keeps the narrative motivation that predicted satisfaction in Ecuador's study, with one avatar, one map and one progress story. Separate mini-games are needed because interests and aptitudes need different mechanics, and because short units fit sessions and allow resume. The recommended count is **six to eight scored mini-games**:

- **Interests:** two choice games, the bazaar swipe deck and the town story. The two give independent evidence and a consistency check.
- **Aptitudes:** three to four adaptive puzzles. Priority goes to spatial, numerical and abstract pattern, then working memory or planning.
- **Stream sims:** four to five optional sims (Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, ICS, I.Com, Humanities, plus Technical/DAE later). They are unlocked after results and act as information and supplementary interest evidence.

This matches the team's four-chapter structure. It trims the puzzle lab from five puzzles to the three or four that can each get enough items to be reliable.

**Durations.** The recommended durations are:

- **Each mini-game: 2–5 minutes.** This includes a 20–30-second practice round, matching pymetrics' 2–3 minutes and the 4.5–6-minute mobile median.
- **Each sitting: 5–10 minutes**, usually two mini-games.
- **Full scored assessment: 25–35 minutes over three to five sittings.** This is close to pymetrics' 25 minutes and O\*NET's 10–20 minutes, far below YouScience's 60–90 and Mindler's 2.5 hours. Students who want to can finish in one or two days. The first results should arrive by about minute 20, after the interest games and two puzzles, with confidence marked "provisional".
- **Results reading and parent sharing: 5–10 minutes.**
- **Stream sims: about 2 minutes each.**
- **Total first-cycle engagement: about 45–60 minutes over one to three weeks.**
- **Later use:** return visits around decision points, such as Class 8/9 results, Matric results and Class 11 admissions, with a **retake after about three months** showing what changed.

Daily-use design is the wrong target for this category.

**Gameplay-only or more features.** Gameplay-only is right for the *assessment*: no questionnaire, consistent with the team's scope. It is wrong for the *product*. Every evidence stream says a recommendation without reasons, information, parents and a next step fails. The meta-analytic guidance literature finds interventions with three or more "critical ingredients" (individual feedback, world-of-work information, modelling, support from the student's network) clearly outperform single-ingredient ones ([CDAA summary](https://cdaa.imiscloud.com/Web/Blog/Posts/What-is-best-practice-in-careers-education--An-outline-of-the-evidence.aspx)). The split is:

- **MVP:** the game world and mini-games; the explained results screen; a stream library with entry tests and careers; the parent share; save/resume, retake and history; Urdu/English; consent; offline sync. Plus a minimal teacher web view with roster, completion, class stream distribution and CSV export, because the team has already committed to a web portal.
- **Later:** flags and the printable one-page summary; a counsellor booking or paid tier; university and programme search; scholarships; short peer and role-model videos; reminders tied to decision dates; regional languages and audio; an LLM rephraser; a teacher training module; alumni mentoring.
- **Avoid:** a long PDF as the main output, streaks, leaderboards, chat and ads.

### Evidence gaps that need primary research

The notes leave a consistent set of holes, and each maps to a specific instrument. Surveys and interviews here are *research methods*. They do not add a questionnaire to the product, and the ethics application should say so.

**Students.** A survey of about 200–400 Grade 9–12 students should be stratified by gender, province, urban/rural and government/private school. It should cover:

- which games they play and for how long, by gender;
- whether they own or borrow a phone, and its brand and RAM;
- Urdu versus English reading preference;
- what they want from a guidance app, including salary, scholarships and role models, all currently unevidenced;
- trust in algorithmic advice;
- how many careers they can name.

Short think-aloud sessions on prototypes should then test three things: whether the art style reads as "childish or cool", whether "game" versus "test" wording changes anxiety, and whether students understand bars versus the hexagon. The pilot itself should measure state anxiety, SUS, completion per chapter and decision self-efficacy before and after.

**Parents.** Interviews of about 15–20 parents, including fathers and mothers of daughters in rural and urban areas, should establish:

- what they need in a report;
- the preferred channel (WhatsApp, PDF or parent-teacher meetings), currently supported only by Gulf data;
- comfort with gameplay data being collected about their child;
- whether OTP consent is workable on shared phones;
- willingness to pay in PKR, for which no data exists.

**Teachers and schools.** Interviews with about 8–12 teachers, coordinators or principals across public, low-fee private and chain schools should cover how stream decisions are actually handled today, the minutes available per class, which flags and reports they would use, and procurement and data-agreement expectations. No Pakistani source describes any of this.

**Technical.** Measure real round-trip latency from Jazz, Zong, Telenor and Ufone in four cities to the UAE and Mumbai regions. Profile touch latency and frame rate on the reference phones. Check Jameel Noori and Gulzar licensing, and confirm the current PDP Bill text on child consent.

**Measurement.** The core validity question remains: agreement between the game-derived RIASEC profile and the O\*NET Interest Profiler benchmark in Pakistani teens. No published teen game has reported this number.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the design question from "which game?" to "how little game is enough, and what must surround it?" The genre answer is modest: familiar casual puzzles and choice cards in a local story world. Modesty here is a strength, because it suits girls, parents, cheap phones and psychometric reliability at once. The harder problems sit outside the games. Rahyaab has to turn a profile into a decision that a family, not a student, makes. It has to work for the many students who borrow a phone. It has to stay playable through shutdowns and load-shedding. And it has to keep telemetry defensible under a law that is not yet passed. A team that builds the parent report and the offline sync as carefully as the puzzles will have a product no Pakistani competitor offers.

The second implication concerns evidence. Almost everything about Pakistani *preferences* in these notes is inferred from adults, other countries or vendor material. The FYP's small primary studies are therefore more than a formality. Student, parent and teacher interviews, a device-and-gaming survey, and a pilot reporting completion, anxiety and agreement with the O\*NET benchmark would be the first published evidence of what Pakistani teens and families want from a game-based stream advisor. That is a contribution in its own right, beyond the app.
