# Turning mini-games into trustworthy stream advice

The strongest design for this app is a **gamified RIASEC interest measure plus a few performance-based aptitude puzzles (spatial, numerical, logical)**, scored by a **transparent, rule-based model** that respects hard eligibility rules (PMDC, PEC, Biology-at-Matric) and always returns a **top-3 shortlist with a Plan B**, never a single verdict. The evidence supports this mix. Vocational interests are fairly stable by the teenage years and predict school performance and persistence. Spatial and math ability in adolescence predict STEM outcomes. Gamified questionnaires keep their validity better than fully "stealth" games, which converge with self-report at only about r = .52. The market gap is real. Pakistan has no scaled, game-based, mobile-first stream advisor: local players are questionnaire websites that upsell counsellors, and major edtechs (Maqsad, Taleemabad, Sabaq, Edkasa) show no career-assessment product. The pain is real too: **69.5% of first-year MBBS students at one university cited parental pressure** as their main reason for choosing medicine, and **about 140,000 candidates competed for about 22,000 MDCAT seats in 2025**. Three constraints shape the MVP. No Urdu RIASEC instrument has a confirmed fit to Holland's structure in Pakistan, so local piloting is required. Cheap Android phones make millisecond reaction-time scoring unfair. Pakistan has no enacted data-protection law, so the app should build to UK Age Appropriate Design Code and GDPR-style standards from day one. Parents, who make most stream decisions, need their own view of the results, not an afterthought.

## Pakistan locks students into streams at 14 with little guidance

The first real fork comes in Class 9, not after Matric. Under the revised national SSC scheme, applied to exams from 2025, the Science group takes Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry plus **Biology or Computer Science**. The Humanities group takes core subjects plus three electives ([AKU-EB SSC Scheme 2025](https://examinationboard.aku.edu/learning-materials/Documents/SSC%20Scheme%20of%20Studies%202025%20and%20Onwards.pdf)). This makes Biology-vs-CS a quiet gate to medicine: choosing CS effectively closes the MBBS route, because HSSC Pre-Medical builds on SSC Biology. That point is inferred from the group structure, not confirmed as a board rule, so verify it per board. A technical route also exists. DAE admission through Punjab TEVTA requires Matric Science with Physics, Chemistry, Maths and English, which means a Humanities Matric student usually cannot enter DAE ([result.pk TEVTA DAE 2026](https://www.result.pk/educom/tevta-dae-admissions-2026-details-out-61718.htm)). The app should give Class 9 users a three-way choice (Science-Biology, Science-Computer, Humanities, plus Technical where available) and spell out which doors each choice keeps open.

At HSSC the options widen: FSc Pre-Medical, FSc Pre-Engineering, several ICS variants (Physics, Statistics or Economics with Maths and CS), General Science, I.Com, and FA with elective bundles. BISE Lahore lists permitted elective combinations such as Maths+Physics+CS and Economics+Maths+CS ([BISE Lahore Study Scheme 2024-26](https://www.biselahore.com/downloads/notifications/misc/StudySchemeInter_2024-26.pdf); [LCWU Intermediate](https://lcwu.edu.pk/inter-colege-admission.html)). Professional councils set the binding downstream rules. MBBS and BDS need **60% in FSc Pre-Medical** plus MDCAT passes of 55% and 50% ([ilmkidunya PMDC clarification](https://www.ilmkidunya.com/edunews/pmdc-clarifies-fsc-marks-requirement-for-mbbs-bds-admissions); [SZABMU](https://admission.szabmu.edu.pk/public/eligibility.php)). Engineering needs 60% in HSSC. **ICS students are eligible for all engineering programs** ([Eduvision](https://www.eduvision.edu.pk/edu_news/pec-declares-ics-students-eligible-for-admissions-to-all-engineering-programs-news-3883)), and Pre-Medical students can switch to engineering with Additional Mathematics ([Eduvision](https://eduvision.edu.pk/edu_news/pec-allows-pre-medical-students-to-seek-admissions-to-engineering-programs-news-4086)). A-Level students need IBCC equivalence, with at least 60% in pre-medical subjects for MBBS ([RMI Admissions](https://admissions.rmi.edu.pk/admissions-2025-26/)). Switching therefore works mainly in one direction: Pre-Medical students can move toward engineering and computing with extra maths, but no route leads back into medicine without Biology.

These decisions are made with little professional help. In one study, **only 36.6% of high-school students had any school career counselling, and 82.6% relied on parents' advice** ([IJCMPH, Arif et al.](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/1435)). Pakistani practitioners describe counselling as neglected, with little local tool development and heavy reliance on foreign theories ([Abidi & Malik 2020](https://greenwichjournals.com/index.php/NH/article/view/444)). The results show up downstream. At Nishtar Medical University, **69.5% of first-year MBBS students cited parental pressure versus 23.6% passion**, and 42.5% were dissatisfied ([JPMA 2025](https://www.jpma.org.pk/index.php/public_html/article/view/20903)). A study of 1,503 Pakistani graduates using the O*NET Interest Profiler found RIASEC-congruent choices in pure sciences, fine arts and psychology, but **not in computer science, electrical engineering, management or accounting** ([Faize & Husain 2024, e-mentor](https://www.e-mentor.edu.pl/eng/article/index/number/106/id/1675)). The funnel math is stark. **140,071 candidates registered for MDCAT 2025 for roughly 22,000 seats** ([PMDC press release](https://pmdc.pk/Documents/press/Press%20release%20MDCAT%202025.pdf)), so about 5 in 6 will not get a medical or dental seat. In Punjab, 36,702 of 50,461 candidates were female ([Daily Pakistan](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/26-Oct-2025/uhs-mdcat-2025-check-paper-and-answer-keys-for-26-october-2025)), so the Pre-Medical squeeze falls hardest on girls' families.

The labour market adds a reason to widen students' horizons. IT exports reached about **$4.6bn in FY26 (+21%)** ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2647548/pakistan)). Gallup Pakistan reports **32.8% unemployment among graduates aged 20-24, and 42.3% for young women graduates** ([Gallup Pakistan](https://galluppakistandigitalanalytics.com/2026/07/24/in-pakistan-a-university-degree-can-mean-a-longer-wait-for-a-job-not-a-faster-one/)). Gallup's baseline differs from PBS figures. A degree alone is not a safe bet, which argues for showing skill-linked routes such as ICS, DAE and allied health as first-class options rather than fallbacks.

## RIASEC interests and spatial-numerical aptitude form the defensible core

**Holland's RIASEC model should be the backbone.** A meta-analysis found interests already substantially stable at ages 12-18, with rank-order coefficients around .55-.58 (primary source: Low, Yoon, Roberts & Rounds 2005, *Psychological Bulletin* 131(5), a meta-analysis of longitudinal studies; found here through a secondary report, [Frontiers 2025 citing Low et al. 2005](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1498424/full)). A 60-study meta-analysis found that interests predict performance and persistence in school and work, with person-environment fit measures predicting best ([Nye et al. 2012](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Vocational-Interests-and-Performance-A-Quantitative/9984380553002771)). RIASEC also maps cleanly onto Pakistan's streams. No published Pakistani mapping exists, so the app's mapping must be designed and then tested.

Licensing decides which items you can use. The O*NET Interest Profiler is free but **not public domain**. Unmodified copies fall under CC BY-ND 4.0, and gamifying, translating or rewording it requires the O*NET Tools Developer License, which obliges the developer to **validate** every modified product ([O*NET license](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_tools.html)). IPIP items, by contrast, are explicitly public domain and may be edited and translated for any purpose ([IPIP](https://ipip.ori.org/)). Public-domain RIASEC markers built from that tradition include the Basic Interest Markers and the AFPD markers, which come in matched **interest and self-efficacy** forms ([Illinois Experts](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/development-and-initial-validation-of-brief-public-domain-riasec-/); [AFPD Spanish version](https://repositoriosdigitales.mincyt.gob.ar/vufind/Record/CONICETDig_e7b17e37d49451bac2ef942e8858bcce)). The practical recommendation is to **build the swipe deck from IPIP/AFPD items, localized to Pakistani activities**, and to use the O*NET Mini-IP's 30 items (5 per type, designed for mobile) as a benchmark in validation studies ([O*NET Mini-IP](https://www.onetcenter.org/reports/Mini-IP.html)). O*NET occupation codes can still bridge streams to careers.

**Aptitude puzzles should focus on spatial and numerical reasoning.** In Project Talent and SMPY data, adolescent spatial ability was a salient predictor of STEM degrees and occupations ([Wai, Lubinski & Benbow 2009](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/iq/high/smpy/2009-wai.pdf)). Spatial ability at age 13 predicted creative achievement 30 years later, beyond math and verbal scores ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/early-spatial-reasoning-predicts-later-creativity-and-innovation-especially-in-stem-fields.html)). The obvious open item bank, ICAR (matrix reasoning, 3D rotation, letter-number series), is **restricted to academic use and excludes commercial websites**, has no Urdu verbal items, and its developers discourage individual IQ-style scoring ([ICAR project page](https://sms.statistik.tu-dortmund.de/en/research/projects/completed-research-projects/international-cognitive-ability-resource-icar/)). A commercial app therefore needs its own puzzle bank, or a university partnership if it wants to borrow ICAR for validation. A Malaysian Rasch study found ICAR items too hard for local respondents ([UKM](https://spaj.ukm.my/ppppm/jpm/article/download/616/474)), so difficulty must be calibrated for Pakistani 14-16-year-olds.

**Use the rest of the psychometric toolkit sparingly.** Conscientiousness robustly predicts academic performance independently of intelligence ([Poropat 2009](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/6bf37c65-b7fa-5f87-9046-89ba519cf530); [Mammadov 2022](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2021-mammadov.pdf)), but it says little about *which* stream fits. Big Five traits belong in advice framing (study habits, the people side of medicine), not in stream scoring. Social Cognitive Career Theory adds a high-value layer: in 796 Italian high-schoolers, self-efficacy and outcome expectations predicted interests, and family supports and barriers acted mainly through self-efficacy ([Univ. Padova](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/2461959)). Measuring RIASEC self-efficacy alongside interest lets the app separate "not interested" from "interested but unconfident." That distinction matters for girls considering engineering or CS, where patriarchal norms shape self-perceived ability ([Mujtaba & Reiss 2015](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1474624/)). The app should leave out Multiple Intelligences and learning styles, even though both are popular in South Asian schools. Learning-style "meshing" fails proper crossover tests ([Frontiers 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01538/full)), and MI lacks validating data ([Waterhouse 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1217288/text)). Explaining why the app skips them builds credibility with counsellors.

**Local validity is the biggest scientific risk.** The only published Urdu RIASEC adaptation, Career Key Urdu (N=179, Karachi and Lahore), had acceptable reliability, but **Holland's hexagonal structure did not fit** ([Hussain et al. 2014, PJPR](https://pjpr.scione.com/cms/fulltext.php?id=284)). A Portuguese secondary-school sample also showed poor circular-order fit ([Univ. Coimbra](https://estudogeral.uc.pt/handle/10316/107258?locale=en)). The app should therefore score the six dimensions independently, avoid hexagon-based "consistency" indices, and run a bilingual pilot with Matric and Intermediate students before treating any score as norm-referenced. Urdu Big Five measures exist, including a 25-item adolescent questionnaire for ages 13-18 ([PJHSS](https://www.internationalrasd.org/journals/index.php/pjhss/article/download/2779/1855/15257)), but no validated Urdu adolescent RIASEC measure was found.

## Gamify the questionnaire, make the aptitude a real game

The evidence points to a clear trade-off. A systematic review found that game-related assessments that **resemble traditional tests show better reliability and validity, while more game-like ones earn better user reactions**, and that their advantages are "fewer than imagined" ([Ramos-Villagrasa & Fernández-del-Río 2023](https://journals.copmadrid.org/jwop/art/jwop2023a18)). A 2025 meta-analysis put the convergence between game-based measures and self-report personality at a pooled **Fisher's Z = .516, which is r ≈ .47, with high heterogeneity** (corrected 7 Nov 2026: the paper reports Z, not r) ([Fadillah et al., IJSG](https://journal.seriousgamessociety.org/~serious/index.php/IJSG/article/download/1028/636/6860)). HireVue's game-based cognitive battery reached convergent r = .50 and test-retest r = .68 ([Leutner et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.942662/full)). These numbers are respectable, but they fall short of the .80 reliability benchmark buyers expect ([Selection Lab](https://www.selectionlab.com/our-blogs/buying-assessments-10-things-to-watch-out-for)). The resolution is a hybrid. Wrap validated interest items in game narrative so the core RIASEC scores stay trustworthy. Use puzzles for aptitude, where performance is the evidence and a game format is natural. Reserve purely behavioural "stealth" inference for low-weight signals until local data validate it.

There is encouraging early evidence that interest games can work. A 2026 interactive-narrative serious game reportedly produced **correlations above .75 with a traditional RIASEC test for all six types** and reduced students' sense of being evaluated. That finding comes from a doctoral-workshop abstract with unverified sample and peer-review status ([UA JDI 2026](https://web.ua.es/va/phdinf/documentos/jdi-2026/rommeljairgutierrezyepez.pdf)). Gamification also carries a known bias: in a gamified Big Five study, **Conscientiousness scores ran substantially higher than on the questionnaire** ([PLoS ONE 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11065274/)). This is a warning for an audience under pressure to look like "doctor material."

For rigor, Evidence-Centered Design is the right scaffold. Stealth assessment rests on Mislevy's ECD and links a competency model, an evidence model and a task model, typically scored with Bayesian networks ([Shute & Rahimi](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/saecdpreprint.pdf); [Kim, Almond & Shute 2016](https://myweb.fsu.edu/vshute/pdf/IJT.pdf)). For the MVP this means a small competency model: six RIASEC interests, three or four aptitudes, a handful of work styles. It also means a Q-matrix mapping every task to what it measures, at least three independent observables per competency, and a check against a short traditional measure before any score is trusted. The pymetrics-style "train on incumbents" approach is wrong for teens. There are no incumbents, and it would reproduce existing gender segregation. Its fairness audit checked only the four-fifths rule ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/02/11/1017955/auditors-testing-ai-hiring-algorithms-bias-big-questions-remain/)). HireVue applicants complained that abstract tasks lacked relevance ([Leutner et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.942662/full)), so the app should skin puzzles in recognisable Pakistani careers and settings.

| Construct | MVP game design | Scored observables | Weight in stream score |
|---|---|---|---|
| RIASEC interests | "Career Bazaar" swipe deck of 60-90 localized activity cards (run a dukaan, repair a motorbike, treat patients at a BHU, code an app), with a super-like | Like, dislike and super-like proportions per type; consistency across parallel cards | High |
| RIASEC self-efficacy | Same cards revisited: "Could you learn to do this?" | Confidence per type | Medium (moderates advice) |
| Interests (behavioural) | Choose which "career world" to visit next; day-in-the-life mini-stories | Free choices, voluntary replays, normalized for task length | Low until validated |
| Spatial | Rotate and fold puzzles, map routes ("build the bridge") | Accuracy on first attempt, by difficulty | High for Pre-Eng and ICS |
| Numerical | Budget a shop, split a bill, pattern series | Accuracy per difficulty level | High for Pre-Eng, ICS and I.Com |
| Logic and planning | Tower-of-Hanoi or pipe puzzles | Moves over optimal, resets | Medium |
| Verbal (Urdu/English) | Word and analogy puzzles | Accuracy | Medium for FA and Pre-Med |
| Judgement and social | Branching "clinic," "classroom" or "client" scenarios (SJT-style) | Choice against a counsellor-keyed answer | Low to medium |
| Persistence and risk | Optional hard levels; a BART-style "grow the crop" game | Retries after failure; adjusted pumps | Explanatory only |

The Balloon Analogue Risk Task has a test-retest reliability of .77 in adults and adolescent validity evidence ([Stony Brook](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/test-retest-characteristics-of-the-balloon-analogue-risk-task-bar/); [BART-Y](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/reliability-and-validity-of-the-youth-version-of-the-balloon-anal/)). It did not replicate among rural Ugandan adolescents ([Uçar thesis](https://unitesi.unipv.it/retrieve/4d44d8b6-64fd-404a-85cf-89013e742ff4/Merve%20_u%c3%a7ar_thesis%20%281%29.pdf)), which is why risk and persistence should explain results rather than drive stream ranking.

Three engineering rules keep the games fair. First, **do not score raw reaction times across students' phones**. A robot study of 26 phones found **device latencies of 50-200 ms and about 105 ms of between-device variability**, enough to swamp individual differences ([Behav Res Methods 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35953659/)). Use accuracy, choices and within-person RT contrasts against a calibration block, and log device model and frame drops. Second, **score the first attempt** of each aptitude puzzle and use replays only for engagement and persistence, because replayable games inflate ability. Third, **remove test cues**. Split the profile into 3-7-minute sessions over several days, use a world map and "unlock a career world" progression, and avoid timers on interest tasks, scores out of 100 and right/wrong feedback. Reward participation, never particular answers. Mindler's battery takes about 2.5 hours ([MakeMyCareer.AI](https://www.makemycareer.ai/guides/best-career-guidance-platforms-2026)), and researchers built gamified psychometrics precisely because long tests cause anxiety and tedium ([UiTM TraitsNavigator](https://ir.uitm.edu.my/id/eprint/126857/1/126857.pdf)). Total play under about 40 minutes is a genuine differentiator.

## Recommend through transparent rules, eligibility gates and a Plan B

Without outcome data at launch, the recommender should be a **versioned linear scoring model**. Each stream's score is the sum of trait scores multiplied by a counsellor-built weight matrix, plus academic fit (Matric marks in the relevant subjects) and a small stated-preference term. Hard eligibility gates, such as Matric CS ruling out Pre-Medical, then filter the list. A linear model makes explanations exact: "ICS ranks first mainly because of your logic-puzzle accuracy and your preference for building and tech cards." Store the per-trait contribution vector with every recommendation for audit. Keep the weights server-side so pilots can tune them without an app release. Move to gradient boosting with SHAP explanations only once consented outcome data exist, such as the stream chosen and satisfaction and grades 6-12 months later. Collaborative filtering ("students like you thrived in...") should stay secondary and be audited by gender, province and urban/rural, because it would otherwise learn today's prestige-and-gender patterns. The architecture is the research team's inference, not a sourced standard, but it is the defensible default. The only Pakistani prototype found, the open-source Career Compass, uses a similar fixed weighting of **40% interest, 35% aptitude and 25% Pakistan-landscape alignment** ([GitHub](https://github.com/ikhosa/Career-Compass-Pakistan)).

The starting trait-to-stream matrix below is a heuristic synthesized from RIASEC logic and the council rules. No Pakistani study validates it, so the pilot must test it.

| Stream | Primary interest signals | Aptitude signals | Hard gates and key facts to show | Plan B doors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FSc Pre-Medical | Investigative + Social | Verbal/memory, science reasoning | SSC Biology; 60% FSc and MDCAT 55% for MBBS; ~140k candidates for ~22k seats | Pharm-D, DPT, Nursing, Allied Health, Biosciences; Additional Maths unlocks engineering and CS |
| FSc Pre-Engineering | Investigative + Realistic | Spatial + numerical | 60% HSSC for PEC programs | BSCS, Architecture, Physics/Maths, BBA |
| ICS | Investigative + Conventional | Numerical + logic | Eligible for all PEC engineering; no medicine | BSCS/SE/AI/DS, BBA, Economics |
| I.Com | Conventional + Enterprising | Numerical | Business and accounting routes | BBA, B.Com, CA, ACCA, LLB |
| FA (elective-specific) | Artistic / Social / Enterprising | Verbal | Closes medicine, engineering and most CS | LLB, Psychology, Media, Fine Arts, Education, CSS |
| DAE / Technical | Realistic, hands-on | Spatial | Requires Matric Science; 60% DAE allows relevant engineering | Direct technician jobs, B.Tech |
| A Levels | Map to the same subject bundles | Same | IBCC equivalence, with 60% in pre-medical subjects for MBBS | Same as the matching FSc stream |

The output screen should show **three ranked fits with confidence bands**. For each, it should show why it fits, the doors it opens and closes, the entry thresholds, and a Plan B. This follows the eligibility evidence above. It also follows the UK Age Appropriate Design Code, which expects profiling of under-18s to come with **age-appropriate explanations and prompts to talk to an adult** ([Lewis Silkin](https://lewissilkin.com/en/insights/ico-issues-final-text-of-age-appropriate-design-code-for-online-services-to-keep-children-safe)). The app should actively counter prestige bias by presenting FA, I.Com and DAE with concrete outcomes, and by showing girls in engineering and CS. In India, girls were about 20 percentage points less likely to take Science or Commerce even after controlling for test scores ([Sahoo & Klasen, IIMB](https://research.iimb.ac.in/fac_pubs/86)), and the same pattern is plausible in Pakistan.

An LLM can turn the computed result into warm Urdu or English prose, but **it must never decide the recommendation**. It should receive only de-identified trait scores, ranked streams and contribution values. It should be constrained to explain those numbers without deterministic or gendered language, and its output should be checked so it mentions only the streams provided. A human-written template should serve as a fallback. Calls should run server-side so API keys stay off the device, and the provider's terms for minor end users should be checked before launch.

**Parent involvement is a core feature, not an add-on**, given that 82.6% of students rely on parents and about 30% are "clearly not allowed to choose" their own career ([ResearchGate](https://researchgate.net/publication/354355591_Career_Counselling_Approaches_in_Pakistani_Students)). The MVP should include a shareable, Urdu-first parent report (a WhatsApp link) built around three things. It should show the child's evidence-based strengths. It should state the realistic odds for medicine. It should show credible alternatives with earnings and growth signals, such as IT exports. The report should end with a structured "talk together" checklist and a prompt to consult a teacher or counsellor. Mindler's school parent-orientation sessions show the B2B form of the same idea ([Jaipuria](https://sajk.jaipuria.edu.in/mindler-student-parent-orientation-session/)). One Pakistani study suggests peers sometimes outweigh family ([INTED 2011](https://library.iated.org/view/ZAFAR2011ACA)), so a peer-comparison-free sharing feature for friends is a reasonable later test. No Pakistani study has measured how families respond to aptitude-based recommendations, so the parent report itself needs user research.

## Expo, Skia and Supabase fit low-end Android and privacy-first design

**Start on Expo SDK 55** (React Native 0.83, React 19.2). The New Architecture is mandatory there ([Expo SDK 55](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-55)), and Reanimated 4 and FlashList 4 require it ([Expo upgrade guide](https://expo.dev/blog/upgrading-to-sdk-55)). Use development builds through EAS rather than Expo Go, and EAS Update to push content and tuning changes. For games, the combination of **react-native-skia, Reanimated 4 (with react-native-worklets) and Gesture Handler** covers swipe decks, puzzles and story choices. Software Mansion recommends Reanimated plus Skia over rendering many React components for complex animation ([Reanimated performance guide](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/guides/performance)). Skia costs roughly **4 MB** of Android download size ([RN Skia install docs](https://mintlify.com/shopify/react-native-skia/getting-started/installation)). Rive's native libraries measured about **12.3 MB across four ABIs** in a Wikimedia prototype ([Phabricator T433250](https://phabricator.wikimedia.org/T433250)), so pick Lottie for decorative animation unless state-driven characters justify Rive. Skip Unity, Phaser-in-WebView and physics engines: none of the planned games need them, and they would cost size, memory and input latency on 2-3 GB phones. That last point is engineering judgment and should be confirmed with a prototype on Infinix, Tecno or Redmi-class devices. For any timed task, take stimulus timestamps from UI-thread frame callbacks and responses from Gesture Handler worklets, using a monotonic clock.

**Supabase is the pragmatic backend.** It provides Postgres, auth, row-level security and Edge Functions. The free tier pauses projects after a week of inactivity and caps the database at 500 MB, so production needs **Pro at $25/month** ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing.md)). On the device, an append-only **expo-sqlite event table with an outbox** that batch-uploads idempotently when online handles patchy connectivity ([React Native Relay 2026](https://reactnativerelay.com/article/building-offline-first-react-native-apps-2026-expo-sqlite-drizzle-orm-sync-strategies)). Scientific telemetry belongs in your own Postgres, with raw events kept so scores can be recomputed as evidence rules change. PostHog's free tier (1M events and 1M feature-flag requests per month) handles funnels and A/B tests of onboarding and deck length ([PostHog pricing](https://posthog.com/pricing)), with session replay disabled for minors. For Urdu, React Native's `I18nManager` RTL switch **takes effect only after an app restart** ([reactnative.dev](https://reactnative.dev/docs/i18nmanager)). Use logical start/end styles, i18next, Noto Nastaliq Urdu for reading text with generous line height, and a reload on language change. Offering Roman Urdu is a reasonable bet that user research should confirm.

**Privacy must be designed to a standard Pakistan has not yet legislated.** As of 2026, Pakistan's Personal Data Protection Bill remains unenacted ([Chambers 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/pakistan); [Commoner Law](https://commoner-law.com/pakistan/data-privacy-digital-rights/data-privacy-without-pdp-act)). PECA 2025 targets online content, which is one more reason to ship **no open chat or user-generated content** ([ARY](https://arynews.tv/peca-amendment-act-2025-the-key-points/)). The UK AADC treats everyone under 18 as a child and expects **profiling off by default** unless it serves the child's best interests ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/data-protection/ico-age-appropriate-design-code-for-information-society-services/)). The recommendation itself is that best-interest purpose. Research use and analytics should be separate opt-ins that default to off. On Google Play, declaring target ages of **13-15 and 16-17** avoids the full under-13 Families program while teen content rules still apply. Youthful store art can trigger review ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)). The Play Age Signals API is rolling out globally in late 2026 ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/07/google-play-age-signals-api-safer-experiences.html)). Concretely, the MVP should use a neutral date-of-birth gate. It should obtain parental consent through an OTP link sent by SMS or WhatsApp, with a plain-language notice in Urdu and English and a timestamped, versioned consent record. It should collect nicknames instead of names and no CNIC or B-form number, precise location, contacts or photos. It should key records by pseudonymous UUID, with parent contact details in a separate restricted table. Raw events should be deleted after aggregation, for example after 12 months, and the app should offer in-app data download and deletion. It should carry no ads.

The positioning writes itself: **"Explore before you lock in."** This would be the first Urdu-first, game-based app that recommends a *Pakistani* stream (Pre-Med, Pre-Eng, ICS, I.Com, FA, DAE, or A-Level bundles), shows the MDCAT odds honestly, and brings parents in. Against CareerCoachs, the app should compete on experience and evidence: short play instead of a long form, and published validation pilots instead of an unexplained "AI." Against YouScience, it should compete on localization and price. Pricing should sit at or below the local bottom rung. A free full student result, with a cheap paid parent report or counsellor session priced under CareerCoachs' PKR 1,700, avoids the paywalled-report complaint that dogs CareerExplorer, CareerGuide and others. Global per-seat school pricing (US$4-45) is far beyond Pakistani willingness to pay, which has not been measured. A B2B or B2G channel should therefore look more like Indian Punjab's **free AI career-guidance labs in 25 government schools** ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/punjab/punjab-launches-ai-driven-career-guidance-in-government-schools/)): NGO- or government-sponsored school licences. The most credible threat is a large Pakistani prep edtech adding a career module, which also makes Maqsad and Edkasa the natural distribution partners. They already own the Pre-Med and Pre-Eng audience at exactly the moment students regret their stream.

One competitive asset is under-appreciated: **validation as marketing**. O*NET's Developer License and ICAR's academic-partner clause both push the app toward a partnership with a Pakistani university psychology department. That partnership is also how the app earns local norms, gender and medium invariance checks, and a publishable claim no local competitor can make.

## Conclusion

The hard part of this product is not the games. It is earning the right to say "this stream suits you" to a 15-year-old and their parents in a country with no local validation data, a near-1-in-6 medical funnel and gendered norms. That shifts the MVP's centre of gravity. Less effort should go to clever stealth inference and more to a modest, explainable measurement core: IPIP-based interest cards, first-attempt spatial and numerical puzzles, and self-efficacy. That core should be validated in a 300-1,000-student bilingual pilot against the O*NET Mini-IP, with fairness checks by gender, province, school type, language medium and phone tier. The pilot doubles as the dataset that later unlocks machine-learning scoring.

The second insight is that the real user is a dyad. A recommendation that persuades the student but not the parent changes nothing. The parent report, the honest MDCAT odds and the Plan-B doors are therefore what make the product effective, not marketing extras. Several open questions should be settled during the pilot rather than assumed: Pakistani families' willingness to pay, whether RIASEC's structure holds in Urdu for teenagers, how budget Android devices actually perform, and how families respond to evidence that contradicts their plans.
