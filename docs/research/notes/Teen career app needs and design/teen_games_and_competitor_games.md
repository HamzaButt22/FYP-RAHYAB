# Teen Mobile Games and Competitor Assessment Games (for Rahyaab game selection)

Scope note: complements (does not repeat) `research_notes/Career guidance games app Pakistan/game_based_assessment.md`, which already covers pymetrics paradigms, HireVue validation stats, Cognify, Owiwi, BART reliability, O*NET Mini-IP and the trait-to-mechanic mapping table. Research budget was ~18 tool calls. Several Pakistan-specific questions have no rigorous source; these are flagged clearly.

## 1. Teen mobile gaming in Pakistan / South Asia: genres, titles, gender, sessions, devices

### Takeaway
No representative survey of Pakistani 14-18-year-olds' game preferences was found. Weaker sources (blogs, Play chart snapshots, small university surveys) agree on a core set: battle royale (PUBG Mobile, Free Fire), board/social games (Ludo King, Ludo Star, Yalla Ludo, Carrom), endless runner (Subway Surfers), and casual puzzle. Evidence on girls is thin. Indian survey data points to casual/puzzle (Candy Crush, word puzzles) for women, and Pakistani focus groups note gender differences without game-level detail.

### Cited Findings
- Blog and marketing roundups for Pakistan, informal and not surveys, consistently name PUBG Mobile, Free Fire and Ludo titles. One lists Ludo, Carrom, PUBG Mobile, Free Fire and Teen Patti Gold as the most played. Free Fire is described as popular "especially among students" because of quick play and low data use. Subway Surfers, Mobile Legends and Roblox are also mentioned. — [Pak Fun Games 2025](https://pakfungames.blog/most-popular-mobile-games-in-pakistan-2025/); [Arena Lobby 2026](https://www.arenalobby.pk/top-mobile-games-pakistani-gamers-love-in-2026/); [gameapp.pk 2026](https://gameapp.pk/top-10-free-mobile-games-pakistan/)
- In an Appfigures snapshot of Google Play Pakistan's top games, Yalla Ludo ranked #1 and Ludo King #3. This is a point-in-time chart, not an age-specific one. — [Appfigures Google Play Pakistan games](https://app.appfigures.com/top-apps/google-play/pakistan/games)
- Sensor Tower, June 2025: the racing game Moto Race Go was #1 globally for download growth and #8 for downloads, with "strong momentum across India, Brazil, and Pakistan". Free Fire was #2 worldwide for downloads in June 2025 and #3 in October 2025, behind Block Blast! (#1) and Roblox (#2). Subway Surfers was in the top 5. — [Sensor Tower June 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/top10-worldwide-mobile-games-revenue-downloads-june-2025); [Sensor Tower Oct 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-october-2025)
- Ludo King (June 2023): Pakistan accounted for 3.1% of downloads and India for 83%. — [GameDev Reports / Sensor Tower June 2023](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-top-mobile-games-by-3ac)
- A Tenjin 2025 report on Pakistan surveyed 50 Pakistani publishers about their genre focus, not player preferences. — [Tenjin 2025](https://tenjin.com/blog/tenjin-the-state-of-mobile-gaming-in-pakistan-in-2025/)
- Play time among Pakistani students, from small, mostly male convenience samples:
  - Lahore educational institutions (N=216): 46.8% play PUBG 1-2 hours a day and 9.7% play more than 5 hours. — [Khan et al., JOSSAMS 2021 / Asian Online Journals](https://www.asianonlinejournals.com/index.php/AJSSMS/article/download/1815/1543)
  - Sargodha University: students were mostly male; heavy PUBG use was linked to stress and sleep problems. — [JMES Sargodha](https://jmes.pk/index.php/JMES/article/download/9/9/36); [JPSA PUBG and mental health](https://journalpsa.com.pk/index.php/JPSA/article/view/442)
  - Bahria Karachi (ages 18-30): PUBG players reported 6-7 hours a day. This is an outlier and self-reported. — [ResearchGate: PUBG addiction case study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/368917061_Addiction_of_Violent_Video_Gaming_among_Pakistani_Players_A_Case_Study_of_Pubg_Game)
- Pakistani adolescents (PAFMJ 2021, qualitative focus groups): Ludo and PUBG came up among online activities, with "considerable gender and age-related differences" in online preferences. The snippet does not give a breakdown by game. — [PAFMJ: Online preferences of Pakistani adolescents](https://www.pafmj.org/PAFMJ/article/view/12207)
- A NUST dissertation (30 interviews) found Pakistani players motivated by escapism, virtual relationships, leisure and entertainment. It also reported gendered sexism and toxicity in online gaming. — [NUST dissertation, Komal Salman 2022](https://nust.academia.edu/KomalSalman)
- Indian women (CMR survey, N=2,000 smartphone users): the top games were Candy Crush (39%), Clash of Clans (22%) and word puzzles (22%), and 94% prefer free-to-play casual games. — [Beebom / CMR](https://beebom.com/women-prefer-candy-crush-pubg-mobile-report/amp/); [CMR India](https://cmrindia.com/the-future-of-mobile-gaming-is-female-cmr/)
- A first-person piece in the Express Tribune describes a Pakistani female gamer's experience; it is anecdotal. — [Tribune: Confessions of a gamer girl](https://tribune.com.pk/article/7321/confessions-of-a-gamer-girl?amp=1)
- A Lahore undergraduate sample (two-thirds female) averaged 6.94 hours of phone use a day (SD 4.10). This is general phone use, not gaming. — [PMC: Nomophobia in Lahore undergraduates](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7486428/)

### Inferences
- The familiar common ground for both genders in Pakistan is the board/social casual genre (Ludo, Carrom) and simple casual puzzle/runner games. Battle royale skews male and is unsuitable anyway: it is violent, long-session and data-heavy. A visual language close to Ludo, Carrom and casual puzzle games (bright, tactile, tile/board-based) should feel familiar to girls and boys and to parents.
- "Low data use" and "quick play" are cited as reasons for Free Fire's popularity among students. This supports small APK size, offline-capable mini-games and short rounds on low-end Android phones.
- Many Pakistani girls may game less, or not see themselves as gamers (by analogy with Pew US data below). Framing Rahyaab as "activities/challenges", not "gaming", and avoiding competitive leaderboards may widen appeal.

### Gaps
- No PTA annual report, Sensor Tower, data.ai or Newzoo source with Pakistan-level, age-segmented genre or title data was accessible. Sensor Tower country data is paywalled.
- No Pakistani or South Asian data on session lengths for teens specifically.
- No Pakistani gender-by-game breakdown, and no Carrom/Ludo usage figures by gender.
- No data on teens' device specs in Pakistan (RAM, Android version), found in this pass.

## 2. Global teen preferences, motivations, age-appropriateness, "childish vs cool"

### Takeaway
Pew (US, 2023 survey) shows near-universal play among teen boys, about three-quarters among girls, and phones as a primary device, especially for girls. Fun is the dominant reason to play, and "learning" is a weak one. Quantic Foundry finds that age differences in motivation exceed gender differences. Women tilt toward Completion, Fantasy, Story and Design (customisation). Younger players tilt toward Excitement and Competition.

### Cited Findings
- Pew Research Center (US teens 13-17, survey Sep-Oct 2023, published May 2024), all from the same report: [Pew 2024, Teens and Video Games Today](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/05/09/teens-and-video-games-today/)
  - Who plays: 85% of teens play video games, including 97% of boys and about 75% of girls. Among girls, 81% of 13-14-year-olds play versus 67% of 15-17-year-olds.
  - Gamer identity: 62% of boys and 17% of girls identify as gamers.
  - Daily play: 61% of boys versus 22% of girls.
  - Smartphones are used for gaming by 70% overall, at similar rates for boys and girls. Consoles are used by 73% (about 9 in 10 boys versus 57% of girls).
  - Reasons for playing: 87% of teen players call fun a major reason. Learning is cited by 50% but called major by only 13%. Competing is a major reason for 36% of boys versus 15% of girls.
  - Social play: 89% play with others.
  - Perceived benefits: 56% say gaming helped their problem-solving.
  - Harms: 41% say it hurt their sleep, and 43% have been harassed while gaming.
- An older Pew page (date unclear, possibly 2008) reports that girls average 6 genres and boys 8. Verify before citing. — [Pew ?p=96085](https://www.pewresearch.org/?p=96085)
- Quantic Foundry (Gamer Motivation Profile, self-selected sample):
  - Women score higher on Design (expression and customisation), Fantasy, Story and Completion.
  - Men score higher on Competition and Destruction. The largest gender gap is for Competition, at d = .33 (87% overlap).
  - Age explains about twice as much variance as gender for Competition, and Excitement correlates most strongly with youth (r = -.28).
  - Gender differences are much smaller among Chinese gamers, which suggests the gaps are cultural.
  - Sources: [Quantic Foundry: Gender differences](https://quanticfoundry.com/2015/08/28/gender-differences-in-gaming/); [GDC 2020 player segments](https://quanticfoundry.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/GDC-2020-Slides-Player-Segments-Quantic-Foundry.pdf); [GamesBeat: Chinese vs American gamers](https://gamesbeat.com/how-chinese-and-american-gamers-differ/)

### Inferences
- Avatar customisation (Design), a light story/world (Fantasy/Story) and collectible progress (Completion) appeal across genders. Fast "excitement" micro-rounds suit younger players. Competitive leaderboards appeal mostly to boys and risk test-anxiety framing, so make them optional or omit them.
- Learning is a weak motivator for teens. Framing should lead with fun and self-discovery ("discover your superpower") rather than "assessment".
- Rating target: aim for PEGI 3/IARC "Everyone", with no violence, gambling imagery (note that pymetrics-style money and card tasks could read as gambling), chat or ads.

### Gaps
- No primary PEGI/IARC or Google Play Families/teen policy text was fetched in this pass. No source was found for what teens find "childish vs cool" in art style (no empirical study located). These remain design judgement.
- No Newzoo teen genre data was fetched.

## 3. Genres for 3-7 minute sessions; session length, retention, "test-like vs fun"

### Takeaway
Industry benchmarks put the median mobile session at about 4.5-6 minutes, with top-tier games at 8-9 minutes and 3-5 sessions a day. Puzzle games have the best day-7 retention and stable performance across regions. A 3-7 minute mini-game fits natural mobile behaviour. Game-like formats improve reactions, but the closer to a real game, the weaker the psychometrics (see prior notes).

### Cited Findings
- GameAnalytics Q1 2024 benchmarks:
  - Global median session length was 4.45 minutes, with 4-5 sessions a day across most genres.
  - Puzzle games show the highest D7 retention of any genre and are stable across regions.
  - Puzzle and word games in the Middle East reach about 10 sessions a day.
  - Casino and card games have the longest sessions (about 20 minutes in North America, more than 25 minutes for card games in Europe).
  - Source: [GameDev Reports summary of GameAnalytics Q1'24](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-benchmarks-in-mobile)
- GameAnalytics 2025: median-tier sessions run about 5-6 minutes and top-tier about 8-9 minutes; average session lengths have fallen. — [GameDev Reports: GameAnalytics 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)
- Kazakhstan GCG field trial (hidden assessment, N=1,865 grade-11 students):
  - 92% completed the tutorial.
  - Intrinsic Motivation Inventory (IMI) score 5.6/7, System Usability Scale (SUS) 76/100, state anxiety 1.9/5.
  - Flow predicted mastery after controlling for GPA, and students praised "authentic micro-tasks".
  - Source: [Tokzhigitova et al., Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1643246/full)
- Ecuador RIASEC narrative game: in-game scores correlated above .75 with the traditional RIASEC test for all six types. Narrative (β = .40) and Vocational Impact (β = .38) were the strongest predictors of satisfaction. Venue and sample are unverified. — [Gutiérrez Yépez et al., UA JDI 2026 PDF](https://web.ua.es/va/phdinf/documentos/jdi-2026/rommeljairgutierrezyepez.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested unit: 5-6 mini-games of about 3-5 minutes each, playable across 2-3 sittings (total about 25-30 minutes, comparable to pymetrics). Puzzle/board/tile genres best match both retention evidence and familiar Pakistani games.
- Narrative quality, not only mechanics, drives satisfaction in interest games (Ecuador β = .40). A thin story wrapper (a journey or "career city") is worth its cost.

### Gaps
- No teen-specific data on attention span or session length; benchmarks cover all ages.
- No puzzle-only session-length figure (the full GameAnalytics tables were not accessed).

## 4. Exact games used by competitors

### Takeaway
Competitors split into two families:
- Lab-paradigm mini-game batteries: pymetrics (12 x 2-3 minutes), Arctic Shores (up to about 9 tasks, 20-45 minutes), HireVue (4 games), YouScience (timed aptitude "brain games", 5-15 minutes each).
- Narrative/world games: Knack's Wasabi Waiter, Owiwi missions, Kazakhstan GCG (3-D exploration), the Ecuador RIASEC narrative, the Brazilian RIASEC islands.

Student-facing products (Xello, Mindler) are mostly short questionnaires with gamified UI, not true games.

### Cited Findings
**pymetrics / Harver (hiring; web/mobile; flat minimal 2-D)**
- There are 12 games of about 2-3 minutes each, roughly 25 minutes in total. Source for the full list: [MyConsultingCoach](https://www.myconsultingcoach.com/bcg-pymetrics)

| # | Game | Mechanic | Measures |
|---|------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Balloon | Inflate balloons without popping them; colour-linked patterns | Risk/reward |
| 2 | Tower | Towers of Hanoi-style disk puzzle | Planning |
| 3 | Money Exchange I | Split money with a partner | Fairness |
| 4 | Money Exchange II | Earn, split, then judge whether the split is fair | Fairness |
| 5 | Keypress | Press a key as many times as possible | Effort/speed |
| 6 | Hard or Easy Task | Choose easy/low-pay or hard/high-pay under uncertainty | Risk and effort |
| 7 | Digits Memory | Recall number strings | Working memory |
| 8 | Stop | Respond only to a target shape | Inhibition |
| 9 | Arrows | Direction rules change with arrow colour | Attention |
| 10 | Lengths | Pick the longer of two near-identical shapes | Discrimination |
| 11 | Cards | Draw from decks to learn the paying pattern | Pattern learning |
| 12 | Faces | Match expressions to situations | Emotion reading |

- Note: Rahyaab's current repo already has Bart.js, EasyHard.js, Hanoi.js, Memory.js and Emotify.js, which mirror pymetrics games 1, 6, 2, 7 and 12.

**YouScience (Discovery/Brightpath; US high school; web; timed puzzle "brain games")**
- Numerical Reasoning: identify the pattern in a number string and predict the next number (about 15 minutes).
- Spatial Visualization: picture a 2-D model as a 3-D object or vice versa (about 7 minutes).
- Idea Generation: counts the quantity, not quality, of ideas produced in a set time (about 5 minutes).
- Brightpath report labels include "Numerical Predictor", "3D Visualizer", "Brainstormer" and "Sequential Thinker".
- Sources: [Cobb County YouScience handout](https://media.cobbk12.org/media/WWWCobb/fgg/560/Juniors%20-%20YouScience%20Information-1.pdf); [Durango Herald](https://www.durangoherald.com/?p=162704); [YouScience: Idea generation](https://www.youscience.com/idea-generation/); [UVU 2025 presentation](https://www.uvu.edu/k16/conference/docs/2025/atuvu-speak-powerpoint.pdf); timings from [Peoria Unified flyer](https://www.peoriaunified.org/cms/lib/AZ01924969/Centricity/Domain/3335/ElevateEdAZ_YouScience_Flyer.pdf) (returned 404 on fetch; timings come from the search snippet only)
- No official Brightpath game-by-game page was found; descriptions may predate Brightpath.

**Arctic Shores (hiring; mobile/desktop; abstract task-based)**
- Up to about 9 short interactive tasks in one sitting.
- Formats include arrows, balloons, sequences and forecasting; the employer chooses the tasks.
- Total time is reported variously as 20-35, 35-40 or about 45 minutes.
- A practice task is unscored and used to check the device.
- It measures behavioural dimensions (resilience, learning agility, risk approach) plus some cognitive ones.
- Arctic Shores warns that third-party guides may be inaccurate, and no official task list is published.
- Sources: [Psychometric Success](https://psychometric-success.com/aptitude-tests/test-types/arctic-shores-test); [PracticeAptitudeTests](https://www.practiceaptitudetests.com/testing-publishers/arctic-shores/); [Interview Guys 2026](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/arctic-shores-test-guide/)

**HireVue game-based cognitive assessment**
- Four games: Numerosity, Shapedance, Pathfinder, Singularity (details in the prior notes). — [Leutner et al., Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.942662/full)

**Knack (hiring; mobile; cartoon simulation)**
- Wasabi Waiter: the player is a sushi waiter delivering correct orders as the restaurant gets busier. It involves micro-decisions, prioritising, sequencing and persistence.
- It logs behaviour at millisecond level to infer conscientiousness, emotion recognition and other traits.
- Dungeon Scrawl is a maze and puzzle game.
- Validity is contested. "Meta Maze" could not be verified.
- Sources: [NPR 2013](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2013/12/01/246999632/playing-the-game-to-get-the-job); [Bloomberg 2013](https://brp-prod-bcc.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2013-10-24/new-way-to-assess-job-applicants-online-games-and-quizzes); [HCAmag](https://www.hcamag.com/ca/news/general/the-lighter-side-video-games-wasabi-and-hr/125527)

**Kazakhstan GCG (education; grade 11; web; 3-D exploration)**
- A bilingual (Kazakh/Russian), web-based, gamified 3-D exploration with hidden assessment.
- Event logs are converted into Rasch-weighted mastery scores across five ICT domains: programming, cybersecurity, web, graphics/UX and computer architecture.
- It was run as a 6-week field trial in 45 schools. The abstract does not report levels, art style or session length.
- Source: [Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1643246/full)

**Ecuador RIASEC interactive narrative**
- Each in-game story choice maps to a RIASEC dimension, so items are answered implicitly. OpenAI is integrated.
- Correlation with the traditional test was above .75 for all six types.
- Not confirmed as published in IEEE TLT 2025.
- Source: [UA JDI 2026](https://web.ua.es/va/phdinf/documentos/jdi-2026/rommeljairgutierrezyepez.pdf)

**Brazil RIASEC game**
- Six thematic islands, one per RIASEC type. — [Univali 2026](https://periodicos.univali.br/index.php/acotb/article/view/22308)

**MeTycoon**
- A game-based (tycoon/simulation) approach to career guidance. — [Coventry PurePortal](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/metycoon-a-game-based-approach-to-career-guidance-2/)

**like2be (Switzerland)**
- A serious game for career choice from the University of Basel and Westminster. The PDF and record could not be parsed, so mechanics, duration and results are unverified. — [Unibas PDF](https://bildungswissenschaften.unibas.ch/fileadmin/user_upload/bildungswissenschaften/04_Forschung/Dokumente/Career-Choice-With-the-Serious-Game-Like2be.pdf); [Westminster record](https://westminsterresearch.westminster.ac.uk/item/w4qzv/career-choice-with-the-serious-game-like2be)

**Xello (K-12; web)**
- Matchmaker is a quiz of about 3-4 minutes (Xello's help pages give inconsistent times). Answers correlate with career activities and give a strength-of-match.
- Mission Complete uses the last 49 questions of the Matchmaker trio. A lesson plan references the first 39 questions.
- It is a gamified questionnaire, not a game.
- Sources: [Xello: Students and Matchmaker](https://help.xello.world/en-us/Content/Knowledge-Base/Xello-6-12/Assessments/Students-Matchmaker.htm); [Xello: Mission Complete](https://help.xello.world/en-CA/Content/Knowledge-Base/Xello-6-12/Assessments/Students-Misson-Complete.htm); [Matchmaker lesson PDF](https://help.xello.world/en-us/Content/Resources/PDFs/Prereq-Lessons-6-12/Matchmaker.pdf)

**Mindler (India; classes 8-12)**
- Assesses 5 dimensions (Orientation Style, Personality, Aptitude, Interest, EQ) on 56 parameters. The founder describes it as "interactive activity based".
- No evidence of true mini-games was found.
- Sources: [BW Disrupt](https://bwdisrupt.com/article/mindler-a-technology-driven-system-that-helps-students-to-choose-the-career-path-best-suitable-for-them-98537); [TeamLease job listing](https://www.teamlease.com/jobs/customer-success-associate-jobs-in-delhi-mindler-163672)

### Inferences
- A defensible Rahyaab mix uses three families:
  - Interests: 1-2 choice/narrative or world-based games (swipe deck plus a story journey) for RIASEC.
  - Abilities: 3-4 YouScience-style aptitude puzzles (number pattern, spatial rotation/folding, verbal/word in Urdu or English, sequence/logic). These map best to stream choice (Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, ICS, Commerce, Humanities).
  - Behavioural traits: at most 1-2 pymetrics-style games (Balloon risk, Hard/Easy effort) as secondary signals.
- Total time should be about 25-35 minutes, split across sessions.
- Pymetrics trait games measure hiring-relevant traits (risk, fairness, inhibition) that do not map strongly to academic streams. The repo's current pymetrics clones should be secondary to aptitude and interest games.
- Money and card tasks risk "gambling" perceptions among Pakistani parents and schools. Reskin them (for example, as seeds or crops, or a cricket-run target).

### Gaps
- No official Arctic Shores task list. No details on Cognify or Owiwi mission content beyond the prior notes. No art-style or engine details for GCG. like2be details are unverified. The Ecuador paper's venue is unconfirmed.

## 5. Educational game design for teens (choice, narrative, low text, local context, avatars, ethical rewards)

### Takeaway
Direct evidence gathered in this pass is limited. The supporting points are:
- Narrative quality predicts satisfaction (Ecuador).
- Authentic micro-tasks and a seamless native-language interface were praised (Kazakhstan GCG).
- Hidden assessment kept anxiety low (GCG).
- Customisation, story and completion motivate across genders (Quantic Foundry).

### Cited Findings
- Narrative (β = .40) and vocational impact (β = .38) predicted satisfaction with the RIASEC game. — [UA JDI 2026](https://web.ua.es/va/phdinf/documentos/jdi-2026/rommeljairgutierrezyepez.pdf)
- In the Kazakhstan GCG trial, bilingual (Kazakh/Russian) authentic micro-tasks gave low anxiety (1.9/5), high motivation (IMI 5.6/7) and good usability (SUS 76). — [Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1643246/full)
- Design, Story, Fantasy and Completion motivations are stronger among women; Competition is stronger among men and younger players. — [Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2015/08/28/gender-differences-in-gaming/)
- 43% of US teen players report harassment while gaming. — [Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/05/09/teens-and-video-games-today/)
- Gendered toxicity was reported in Pakistani gaming. — [NUST dissertation](https://nust.academia.edu/KomalSalman)

### Inferences
- Rahyaab should have:
  - A bilingual Urdu/English toggle with icon-led, low-text instructions.
  - Local scenes (bazaar, BHU, cricket ground, workshop, school lab).
  - A customisable avatar with modest dress options suitable for girls.
  - No open chat, and no public leaderboards by default.
  - Rewards for completion only (badges and avatar items), never for particular answers.
  - No loot boxes, streak pressure or ads.

### Gaps
- No primary sources fetched on dark-pattern guidelines (for example, UK Age Appropriate Design Code or Google Play Families policy), on low-literacy UI evidence in South Asia, or on avatar effects in teen assessment.
