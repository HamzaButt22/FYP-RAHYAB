# What secondary students want, value and dislike in career guidance / stream-selection tools (Pakistan first, South Asia, global)

Evidence-strength legend used below: **[SYS]** = systematic (peer-reviewed study, representative or sampled survey, trial); **[SURVEY-NR]** = survey reported via press, non-weighted or vendor-run; **[ANEC]** = anecdotal (opinion piece, blog, forum, review, vendor marketing). Scope note: earlier reports in this workspace already cover competitors, RIASEC/aptitude frameworks, the game-measurement literature and parents-as-second-user; this file avoids repeating those and focuses on student-side needs.

## Q1. What do teens (especially in Pakistan) say they want from career guidance?

### Takeaway
Pakistani secondary students mostly have no guidance at all. The evidence shows unmet needs (help choosing subjects, interest identification, knowing more than a handful of careers) more than stated wish-lists. Students' choices are dominated by parents, siblings and the "doctor/engineer" norm. A Dec-2025 IBCC rule change has made the post-matric decision more open, which raises the value of good information.

### Cited Findings
- **[SYS] KP student survey (n=380 of 30,439 secondary students, random sample, validated questionnaire):** the study found "non-availability of career guidance facilities in schools". It reported no trained career guides, no help with subject selection, and no mechanism or assessment tools for identifying students' interests. It recommends trained guides plus subject-selection and career-orientation support. — [JDSS, Evaluation of the Career Guidance Facilities in Secondary Schools of KP](https://ojs.jdss.org.pk/journal/article/view/1435)
- **[SYS] KP teacher survey (n=99, 2024):** teachers rate secondary school as the most important stage for career development. They cite limited materials, seminars and workshops, and no teacher training in guidance. — [SPARK journal, An Assessment of Career Guidance Services at Secondary School Level in KP](https://journal.suit.edu.pk/index.php/spark/article/view/1060)
- **[SYS] Lahore (6 secondary schools, older 2006 thesis):** no formal guidance programme existed. Students reported problems and wanted help resolving them. — [UMT eScholar](https://escholar.umt.edu.pk/items/bdcc7541-b637-4f88-8da4-6cae6b3171de)
- **[SYS] University level, retrospective:** 70% of QAU students had received no career counselling in any institution. — [PIDE thesis](https://thesis.pide.org.pk/thesis/availability-of-career-counseling-in-pakistan-a-case-study-of-quaid-e-azam-university-islamabad/). In a 400-student graduate sample, most were guided by parents rather than a counsellor. — [PJHSSR, Wumardan](https://ojs.wumardan.edu.pk/index.php/pjhssr/article/view/133)
- **[SYS] Influences on subject choice:** an AKU MPhil thesis on O-Level subject choice found parental guidance and school recommendations to be major factors. — [AKU eCommons](https://ecommons.aku.edu/etd_pk_ied_mphil/77). A Punjab University study found siblings' education significantly linked to subject choice, but not father's occupation. — [JIS, Punjab University](https://jis.pu.edu.pk/44/article/download/888/889/1775)
- **[ANEC] Parental / social pressure toward medicine and engineering, and stigma against arts,** is repeatedly described in Pakistani press. — [Dawn letter "Career counselling"](https://www.dawn.com/news/1597563); [Dawn Images, "I escaped society's pressure to become a doctor"](https://images.dawn.com/news/1188088); [Pamir Times opinion](https://pamirtimes.net/2011/08/01/opinion-the-career-choice-dilemma)
- **[SYS-policy] IBCC 183rd meeting (reported 9 Dec 2025):** matric arts students may now enter any intermediate group (pre-medical, pre-engineering, ICS); previously the group was tied to the matric background. Open enrolment is "from the next session". Replacing the matric science/arts split with an SSC with flexible electives was discussed but not decided. — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/12/09/new-admission-policy-announced-for-arts-students-across-pakistan/amp/)
- **[ANEC-news] Punjab has added new matric groups** (health sciences, agriculture, IT, entrepreneurship). — [Geo News](https://www.geo.tv/latest/586495-punjab-introduces-new-groups-for-matric-students)
- **[ANEC] Switching cost is high:** Pakistani prep sites warn that changing an FSc group after starting means repeating two years. They frame the choice by subject liking/strength plus target career (MDCAT for pre-medical, etc.). — [Parhlai, Pre-Medical vs Pre-Engineering](https://parhlai.com/blog/pre-medical-vs-pre-engineering); [Maqsad blog, ICS vs Pre-Engineering](https://blog.maqsad.io/blog/which-is-better-ics-or-pre-engineering)
- **[SURVEY-NR] India, narrow career awareness:** a Mindler survey of ~10,000 students aged 14–21 reportedly found 93% aware of only ~7 careers (law, engineering, medicine, accounts/finance, design, IT, management). The original report was not located; one page cites 80%. — [uLektz news](https://news.ulektz.com/?p=31819); [Education World](https://educationworld.in/helping-children-choose-new-age-career-pathways/)
- **[SYS] India, UNICEF 2023 survey (4,968 students, 25 states):** only 9.36% had received career guidance and knew such services existed. The headline reports that boys had better access to career information than girls. — [The Week / PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2024/07/18/del88-unicef-education-report.html)
- **[SYS] India, ASER 2023 ("Beyond Basics", youth 14–18):** reported youth lacking career clarity and role models. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/education/aser-2023-report-india-youth-lacks-career-clarity-no-role-model-in-life)
- **[SURVEY-NR] Global U-Report (UNICEF, Feb 2020, 40,000 respondents, 150 countries, unweighted):** 31% said the skills/training on offer did not match their career aspirations. — [UNICEF press release](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/third-youth-surveyed-globally-unicef-say-their-education-not-preparing-them-skills)
- **[SURVEY-NR] ETF–UNICEF U-Report career guidance study (11 countries in Europe/Asia, not Pakistan):** "understanding skills and interests" (37.0%) was the most common type of career support received. — [ETF slides](https://www.etf.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-03/Session%201_PPT_12%20March_Final.pdf)
- **[SURVEY-NR] US teens (JA/Citizens 2025, n=1,000, 13–18):** 88% feel pressure to follow a specific educational or career path. — [JA USA press release](https://jausa.ja.org/news/press-releases/shifting-attitudes-new-research-reveals-teens-views-on-ai-career-preparedness-and-the-future-of-work)
- **[SURVEY-NR] Pakistan youth (UNFPA, July 2026, >1,700 young people):** 76% optimistic about the future. UNFPA called for wider access to skills training and decent jobs. — [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/07/14/76-of-young-pakistanis-are-optimistic-about-future-un-report/)

### Inferences
- The core student need in Pakistan is basic: help identifying one's interests and choosing subjects or groups. This is exactly what the KP study says is missing, so an interest-to-stream function meets a documented gap.
- Narrow career awareness (the "7 careers" problem, if similar in Pakistan) implies a requirement for a **career/degree library that widens options** beyond MBBS and engineering, linked to each stream.
- Because switching FSc group costs two years, and arts→science entry is now open, the app should show **consequences per stream**: which degrees, entry tests (MDCAT/ECAT/NUST NET etc.) and careers each group keeps open or closes. It should also show a Plan B.
- Because family pressure is the dominant influence, the requirements should include **shareable, parent-readable output** (this was already covered in earlier reports).

### Gaps
- No nationally representative Pakistani survey of what Grade 9–12 students *say* they want in a guidance app was found. The available evidence is regional (KP, Lahore) and about provision, not preferences.
- Student desire for salary data, scholarship info, Urdu and peer stories in Pakistan is plausible but **was not evidenced** by any source found in this pass.
- Reddit (r/pakistan, r/CBSE, r/Indian_Academia) and Ilmkidunya/Maqsad forum threads did not surface in search, so there is no forum-sourced student voice in these notes.

## Q2. Common complaints about existing career tests and apps

### Takeaway
Systematic evidence of student complaints about specific apps was not found. What exists is expert and vendor commentary: generic or unvalidated tests, results least useful for undecided students, and questions students cannot relate to. Pricing and paywall complaints could not be verified for Mindler or iDreamCareer.

### Cited Findings
- **[ANEC-vendor] CareerExplorer's own blog** argues that career tests help most those who already know their interests and least the undecided. It also says situational questions are useless if test-takers cannot relate to them. — [CareerExplorer, "Why Career Tests Don't Work"](https://www.careerexplorer.com/blog/why-career-tests-dont-work)
- **[ANEC-vendor] Dheya (India)** says generic personality typologies have a poor evidence base for career guidance. Professional bodies (e.g., SIOP) advise against them, and a growing share of tools publish no reliability or validity data ("personality quizzes rebranded as career assessments"). — [Dheya, How Accurate Are Career Assessment Tests?](https://www.dheya.com/insights/career-assessment-accuracy-india)
- **[ANEC] Vendors themselves frame results as "a guide, not a definitive answer"** and advise combining them with real-world exposure. — [The Forage](https://www.theforage.com/blog/basics/career-aptitude-test)
- **[ANEC] iDreamCareer's model:** a psychometric test (aptitude, personality, interest) leads to a personalised report with paths, exams and jobs, plus booking 1:1 counsellor sessions in-app. No pricing or Play Store review data was retrievable. — [Squareboat case study](https://squareboat.com/clients/idreamcareer)
- **Name collision warning:** searches for "Mindler reviews" mostly return the *Swedish psychology app Mindler* and Mindler UK (unrelated to Indian career platform Mindler). Those reviews must not be used as evidence about career-test users. — [Trustpilot Mindler UK](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/mindlercare.com); [Dagens PS](https://www.dagensps.se/tag/mindler/)

### Inferences
- Requirements implied: (a) scenarios and items localised to Pakistani teen life, so students can relate to them; (b) published reliability and validation evidence plus an explanation of the method; (c) an "unsure / exploring" path, because undecided students benefit least from standard tests; (d) results presented as suggestions with reasons, not verdicts.
- The task brief's list of complaints (too long, paywalled reports, exam-like feel, no follow-up, ignoring marks/eligibility) is **plausible but unverified** in this pass. Treat it as a hypothesis for the app's own user testing.

### Gaps
- Could not extract Google Play reviews for Mindler, iDreamCareer, CareerGuide, Maqsad, Taleemabad or Ilmkidunya (Play Store pages are not fetchable as text; search returned no review aggregations).
- No academic study of complaints about South Asian career apps was found.

## Q3. Which features do teens actually use and return to?

### Takeaway
No public feature-level usage data was found for Xello, YouScience, CareerExplorer or Mindler. The best evidence is evaluation and trial findings: digital tools work as preparation for human guidance, students revisit choices non-linearly, and personalised displays increase information-seeking.

### Cited Findings
- **[SYS-in progress] MDRC "CTE Advise" (IES-funded)** is evaluating Xello and YouScience in high schools using administrative data, surveys, interviews and **tool usage data**. Outcomes are CTE course-taking and career awareness; no feature-level results were found yet. — [MDRC project page](https://www.mdrc.org/project/cte-advise-advising-tools-secondary-education); [IES award](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/choice-and-information-impact-technology-based-career-advising-tools-high-school-students-cte)
- **[ANEC-vendor] Xello describes its core content:** career profiles with regional labour-market data and **interviews with people working in the field**, plus Holland-based interest, personality and skills fit, and educator dashboards tracking engagement. — [IES award description](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/choice-and-information-impact-technology-based-career-advising-tools-high-school-students-cte?ID=3401); [How Xello Works](https://xello.world/en/?p=36538)
- **[SYS] Swiss digibe.ch longitudinal study (lower-secondary, 3 years):** career planning was ongoing and non-linear, and students revisited choices. Tool success depended on students' readiness to reflect and on teacher facilitation. — [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/15378908)
- **[SYS-qual] UK Personal Guidance Fund evaluation:** digital tools let students arrive at 1:1 guidance better prepared, helped target scarce counsellor time, and generated useful needs data. — [North East CA evaluation](https://ambition.northeast-ca.gov.uk/downloads/2003)
- **[SYS] IZA DP 18490 field experiment (low-income lower-secondary students):** self-exploration plus a **personalised display** increased career-information seeking. Low performers shifted toward occupations requiring a high-school diploma. Details come from search snippets, because the paper page returned 404. — [IZA citation record](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp18490/1/citations/bibtex)

### Inferences
- Requirements: retake / revisit with history (non-linear exploration); saved careers and shortlists; personalised result screens that link to career and degree profiles; "people like me" interview or video content (Xello's model); an export or summary for counsellor, teacher or parent sessions.
- Design for a counsellor/teacher touchpoint even if optional, because the evidence says tools add value as preparation for human guidance.

### Gaps
- No retention or engagement numbers (e.g., % of students returning, most-viewed features) exist publicly for any product checked.

## Q4. Teen attitudes to AI/algorithmic advice, being "assessed", and test anxiety

### Takeaway
Teens widely use AI and are anxious about AI's effect on jobs. No survey directly measuring teen trust in AI *career advice* was found. Gamification reliably raises enjoyment and motivation, but evidence that it lowers test anxiety is mixed.

### Cited Findings
- **[SURVEY-NR] Scotland (Glasgow Kelvin College, 2026, n=517, ages 13–18):** half had reconsidered career goals because of AI, and 54% see AI as an opportunity. Trades were seen as "AI-proof". — [STV News](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/half-of-scottish-teenagers-say-ai-has-made-them-rethink-career-goals-survey-finds)
- **[SURVEY-NR] UK (BBC Bitesize, 2026):** more than a third of teens fear AI will harm their job prospects, while two-thirds would use AI to help get a job. — [IT Brief UK](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/bbc-bitesize-expands-ai-careers-guide-for-teenagers); [Compare the Cloud](https://www.comparethecloud.net/news/ai-anxiety-teens-36-percent-worry-jobs-bbc-research)
- **[SYS] MDPI Behavioral Sciences (Jan 2026):** teens' familiarity with, trust in and positive attitude toward AI predicted preference for jobs with low AI-replaceability. — [MDPI 16(1):72](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/16/1/72)
- **[SURVEY-NR] Young adults (Harvard Youth Poll, 18–29):** 59% see AI as a threat to job prospects. — [AOL/Harvard Youth Poll](https://www.aol.com/articles/59-young-people-believe-ai-160609675.html)
- **[ANEC-press] India:** experts warn AI cannot replace human career counsellors, citing only ~10% of students receiving guidance. — [ETV Bharat via Seth Associates](https://www.sethassociates.com/29th-september-2025-only-10-of-indian-students-receive-career-guidance-experts-warn-ai-cant-replace-human-career-counsellors-etvbharat.html)
- **[SYS-review] Sonnleitner (2019), gamification of psychological tests:** game mechanics make assessment more enjoyable and increase test motivation, but the effect on performance is unclear. Students *expected* less anxiety, which was an expectation rather than a measured outcome. — [ORBilu PDF](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/41452/1/Sonnleitner_2019_Gamification%20of%20psychological%20tests_three%20lessons%20learned.pdf)
- **[SYS] Null anxiety effect:** a medical-education study (n=251) found no significant test-anxiety difference between gamified and non-gamified assessment, though qualitative data suggested motivational benefit. — [MetaTOC listing](https://metatoc.com/papers/97555-game-based-assessment-investigating-the-impact-on-test-anxiety-and-exam-performance)
- **[SYS-weak] Indonesia (Quizizz, 2019):** students' attitude that Quizizz reduces test anxiety was high (M=3.94), based on self-report only. — [IJER](https://www.ijer.ftk.uinjambi.ac.id/ijer/article/view/92)

### Inferences
- Requirements: show **why** each recommendation was made (traits → stream), state that it is not an exam and has no pass/fail, and avoid timers or score displays that feel like grading. Offer a "talk to a human" route. Add a section on how careers are changing with AI and automation, because teens are actively worried about this.
- Do not claim that the game format reduces anxiety; claim engagement. Measure anxiety in the app's own pilot.

### Gaps
- No data on Pakistani teens' trust in AI or algorithmic advice was found.
- No adolescent-specific head-to-head study of game vs questionnaire anxiety was found.

## Q5. Gender and rural/urban differences in Pakistan (phones, permissions, restrictions)

### Takeaway
Pakistan has the world's widest mobile-ownership gender gap. Many girls and women use borrowed or shared phones, and permission from male relatives is a key barrier. Girls' career choices are shaped by mobility and safety concerns, marriage expectations and family deliberation. This argues for shared-device, privacy and offline-friendly design and for showing girls the full range of options.

### Cited Findings
- **[SYS] GSMA Mobile Gender Gap Report 2026 (2025 data):** 68% of Pakistani women own a mobile phone versus 93% of men, the widest ownership gap among surveyed countries. The smartphone gap narrowed from 48% to 30%, as women's smartphone ownership rose from 30% to 40%. **28% of women mobile-internet users access it only on someone else's phone (men 4%).** — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2649428/pakistan); [Dunya News](https://dunyanews.tv/en/Technology/960525-pakistan-narrows-mobile-internet-gender-gap-report). Another summary gives the ownership gap as 37% to 27%, which conflicts with the arithmetic of the 68/93 figures; check the primary report.
- **[SYS] GSMA 2025 (2024 data):** about 8 million Pakistani women came online in 2024, and the mobile-internet gender gap narrowed from 38% to 25%. — [Profit / Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/05/15/8-million-women-come-online-in-pakistan-in-2024/)
- **[SYS-secondary] Lack of permission from male family members** is a commonly cited reason women do not own phones. Literacy, digital skills and family disapproval are top barriers. — [Gallup Pakistan Digital Analytics](https://galluppakistandigitalanalytics.com/?p=6753)
- **[SYS-qual] Hazara (24 female teachers):** mobility restrictions, family obligations and social expectations steer women toward teaching. — [ORJSS, GCWUS](https://ojs-orjss.gcwus.edu.pk/journal/article/view/45)
- **[SYS-qual] Peshawar medical/dental students (Frontiers in Medicine, 2026):** career decisions were collective family deliberations, and marriage expectations consistently constrained women's planning, with izzat (honour) norms adding distinct barriers. — [Frontiers in Medicine](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2026.1755128/full)
- **[SYS] Mujtaba & Reiss (2015):** patriarchal values shaped women's perceptions of their own abilities and aspirations, and teaching styles were linked to girls leaving the maths/science pipeline. — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1474624/)
- **[SYS-pilot] Counterpoint, Karachi (n=141 female medical students/house officers):** most made their own choice to become doctors. — [JPMA archive](https://www.archive.jpma.org.pk/PdfDownload/8567)
- **[SYS] India, UNICEF 2024 report:** boys have better access to career information than girls. — [The Week / PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2024/07/18/del88-unicef-education-report.html)

### Inferences
- **Non-functional requirements:**
  - Multi-profile or quick log-out on shared devices.
  - Results that are private by default, with student-controlled sharing.
  - Low data use and offline play.
  - Low-end Android support.
  - No requirement for a personal phone number or email, since a school or class code could be used instead.
- **Functional requirements:**
  - Show girls the full set of stream outcomes, with options that fit local constraints (e.g., local or home-city universities, women's colleges, remote work), without assuming restrictions.
  - Include female role models.
  - Provide a parent-facing explanation, since decisions are collective.
- Rural/urban differences specific to Grade 9–12 students were not directly evidenced, so treat them as inferred from the device and access data.

### Gaps
- No Pakistan-specific survey separating rural and urban Grade 9–12 students' guidance needs was found.
- No data on teen girls (under 18) specifically; the GSMA figures are for adult women.
