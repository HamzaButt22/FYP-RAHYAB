# Recommender systems and ML/expert systems for stream, subject and major recommendation

Method note: Papers were found through the OpenAlex API (about 25 queries, 2015-2026 filter) and the arXiv API. Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 403 and was not used. Unless a finding says otherwise, **every detail below comes from the abstract in OpenAlex, not from the full text.** Dataset sizes, metrics and features that an abstract omits are marked "not in abstract". Venue-quality flags are my own assessment from venue reputation and indexing status, so treat them as indicative. "Low-tier/questionable" means the reported numbers should not be relied on without reading and replicating the paper.

## Q1. Which approaches exist for stream, subject, major and career recommendation? (catalogue)

### Takeaway
The literature on stream selection at school level (Bangladesh, India, Sri Lanka, Benin, Thailand, Philippines) is dominated by small-sample (325-800 students) supervised classifiers: decision tree, random forest, Naive Bayes, SVM and XGBoost. They are trained mostly on prior marks plus family and demographic variables, and are almost all published in low-tier venues. The stronger papers (ACM RecSys, IEEE Access, Springer EAIT, Personnel Psychology) cover university-major or career recommendation. I found no peer-reviewed ML or recommender paper on Pakistani FSc/ICS/intermediate group selection.

### Cited Findings

**A. School-level stream selection (closest analogues to Pakistan's classes 9-12)**

1. **Ahmad, Alam, Uddin, Bhuiyan, Apon (2023)**, "Machine Learning based Stream Selection of Secondary School Students in Bangladesh," *Indonesian Journal of Electrical Engineering and Informatics (IJEEI)* 11(1). DOI 10.52549/ijeei.v11i1.4302. Country: Bangladesh, where the Science, Business Studies or Humanities stream is chosen after grade 8 and closely mirrors Pakistan's choice of grade 9 group. Inputs: 26 attributes per student, covering PSC and JSC subject-wise results, parents' education, profession and monthly income, siblings and district. Algorithm: a range of ML regressors. XGBoost performed best for Science and Humanities, and SVM performed best for Business. Dataset size, metric values, explainability and fairness are not in the abstract. Venue: Scopus-indexed regional journal, mid/low tier. — [IJEEI](https://doi.org/10.52549/ijeei.v11i1.4302)
2. **Hossain, M. M. (2022)**, "Beyond Grades: A Machine Learning-Based Decision Support System for Academic Stream Selection in Bangladeshi Secondary Schools," *Data Modeling*. DOI 10.25163/data.3110802. Inputs: 364 students from 3 institutions, with 12 attributes covering academic history, study habits, feared subjects and hobbies. Algorithms: Naive Bayes, SMO (SVM) and random forest. Accuracy was RF 84.9%, NB 82.76% and SMO 80.88%. Flag: the abstract is written as a "review-style study" of "an attempt", which suggests a re-write of someone else's work, and the venue is obscure. Provenance is unclear. — [Data Modeling](https://doi.org/10.25163/data.3110802)
3. **Sethi, Jaiswal, Ansari (2018)**, "Machine Learning Based Support System for Students to Select Stream (Subject)," *Recent Advances in Computer Science and Communications* (Bentham). DOI 10.2174/2213275912666181128120527. Country: India. Inputs: interest, family background, previous education and "information of successful scholars of their stream". Uses data from several institutions covering two streams. Algorithms: several ML classifiers, with a best accuracy of 86.72%. Sample size is not in the abstract. Cited 37 times, the most-cited school-stream paper found. — [Bentham](https://doi.org/10.2174/2213275912666181128120527)
4. **Thakkar, V. (2024)**, "An Enhanced ML based Approach for Stream Selection of Higher Secondary Education in India: CareerX," *Journal of Electrical Systems* (no DOI in OpenAlex). Country: India (Gujarat), choosing a stream after SSC/10th. Inputs: academic (SSC) scores, IQ test results and personality tests, labelled with the course the student chose. The model was first trained on IQ and SSC scores, and personality traits were then added to measure their effect. Results are not in the retrieved abstract. Flag: the journal's scope does not match the topic, and I believe it was discontinued from Scopus around 2024, which I could not verify. Treat as low-tier. — [OpenAlex search result; Journal of Electrical Systems](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=CareerX%20stream%20selection)
5. **Saluja, R. (2024)**, "A Streamlined Approach to Student Stream Prediction Using an Ensemble Machine Learning Model," *Communications on Applied Nonlinear Analysis*. DOI 10.52783/cana.v32.2668. Country: India. Uses a stacked ensemble of DT, KNN, NB and one-vs-rest SVM, with 80% accuracy. Data came from students in "five distinct branches" (post-secondary engineering branches, not school streams). Flag: the venue is widely reported as questionable/paper-mill-associated, and the topic is outside its scope. — [CANA](https://doi.org/10.52783/cana.v32.2668)
6. **Abeywardhane & Gamage (2023)**, "Subject Stream Prediction: A Machine learning Approach to Select the Suitable Subject Stream for Senior Secondary Students in Sri Lanka," Zenodo preprint. DOI 10.5281/zenodo.7525178. Inputs: O/L exam results, skills and preferred career area. Four ML algorithms were compared, and random forest was best with about 72% accuracy. The system returns a ranked top-10 list of streams with linked jobs and qualifications. This is a top-N design that suits an app, but the paper is not peer reviewed. A 2025 follow-up by Abeywardhana & Abeywardhane appeared in *IJISRT* (DOI 10.38124/ijisrt/25nov1533), which is widely regarded as predatory. — [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7525178); [IJISRT](https://doi.org/10.38124/ijisrt/25nov1533)
7. **Houngue, Hountondji, Dagba (2022)**, "An Effective Decision-Making Support for Student Academic Path Selection using Machine Learning," *IJACSA* 13(11). DOI 10.14569/ijacsa.2022.0131184. Country: Benin, at the post-GCSE choice between the Literature, Maths/Physical Sciences and Biology streams. Inputs: average marks in Maths, Physics-Chemistry-Technology and Biology from grades 6-9, for **325 students**. Algorithms: decision tree, random forest, linear SVC and others. It predicts (i) scientific versus literary aptitude and (ii) the stream. The study was motivated by a 42% pass rate in the Biology stream. Final metrics are not in the truncated abstract. IJACSA is Scopus-indexed but low/mid tier. — [IJACSA](https://doi.org/10.14569/ijacsa.2022.0131184)
8. **Makond, B. (2022)**, "A Decision Model to Predict the Selection of Education Streams – A Case Study for Trang Province, Thailand," *TEM Journal* 11(3). DOI 10.18421/tem113-44. **800 year-10 students** (428 general and 372 vocational). Algorithm: decision tree, with high sensitivity and g-mean. Six influential variables: maths GPA, science GPA and overall GPA in junior high, father's education, mother's occupation, and **student gender**. Note that the model predicts what students *did* choose, not what suits them, so it reproduces existing gender and SES patterns. — [TEM Journal](https://doi.org/10.18421/tem113-44)
9. **Lusdoc & Namoco (2019)**, "Selecting an SPA (special program in the arts) major for high school students using AHP combined with interest inventory," *International Journal of Innovative Research in Education*. DOI 10.18844/ijire.v6i1.4118. Country: Philippines. Combines an interest inventory with AHP weighting of criteria such as late realisation of interest, career opportunity and finances. 77% of evaluated students matched the major they were taking. This needs no training data. — [IJIRE](https://doi.org/10.18844/ijire.v6i1.4118)
10. **Mat Surin, E. S. (2015)**, "Academic streaming for secondary schools in Malaysia using a two-step clustering technique," University of Malaya thesis. Uses unsupervised two-step clustering to assign students to the science or arts stream. The Ministry of Education sets a 60:40 science:arts human-capital ratio (2011 policy), which shows that streaming can be partly a policy-driven allocation problem. — [UM Students Repository](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Academic%20streaming%20secondary%20schools%20Malaysia%20twostep%20clustering)

**B. High-school-leaver to university major or career (higher-quality venues)**

11. **Stein, Weiss, Chen, Leeds (2020)**, "A College Major Recommendation System," *ACM RecSys 2020* (late-breaking/short). DOI 10.1145/3383313.3418488. Country: USA (Fordham). Uses **nine years of historical student records** from one university. Inputs: courses taken in the first years and the grades earned in them. Evaluation: whether the actual major is in the top 5, and whether the student performed above average. It strongly outperforms a random baseline, and **students who followed the recommendation were 12% more likely to perform above average**. This approach needs large outcome data and cannot be used at the school cold-start stage. — [ACM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3383313.3418488)
12. **Qamhieh, Sammaneh, Demaidi (2020)**, "PCRS: Personalized Career-Path Recommender System for Engineering Students," *IEEE Access* 8. DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3040338. Country: Palestine. Inputs: academic performance, **personality type** and extra-curricular skills. Algorithm: fuzzy, N-layered architecture. The personality-to-discipline mapping was built from **1,250 engineering students** in 7 disciplines. Delivered as a mobile app. It was evaluated on 177 engineers who were satisfied with their discipline, and showed **only "slight agreement" (low kappa)** with their actual discipline. This is an honest negative result that shows the limits of personality-based matching. 63 citations. — [IEEE Access](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3040338)
13. **Lahoud, Moussa, Obeid, El Khoury, Champin (2022)**, "A comparative analysis of different recommender systems for university major and career domain guidance," *Education and Information Technologies* (Springer). DOI 10.1007/s10639-022-11541-3. Country: Lebanon, with high-school students. Compared user-based and item-based CF, demographic, knowledge-based (case-based reasoning) and ontology approaches, plus hybrids. **The best was a knowledge-based hybrid that combined user-based CF, CBR and ontology.** It found similar cases 98% of the time (95% personalised), with 95% usefulness and 92.5% satisfaction in student feedback. Sample size is not in the abstract. This is the most directly relevant comparative study of recommender families for high-school leavers. — [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-022-11541-3)
14. **Alghamdi, Alzhrani, Algethami (2019)**, "Fuzzy-Based Recommendation System for University Major Selection," *IJCCI 2019* (SciTePress). DOI 10.5220/0008071803170324. Country: Saudi Arabia. Inputs: qualification exam scores, high-school grades and preferences. Algorithm: a fuzzy expert system plus a cluster-based preference technique using a distance measure. Tested with **only 15 prospective students**, measuring usability and satisfaction. — [SciTePress](https://doi.org/10.5220/0008071803170324)
15. **Peker, Gürüler, Şen, İstanbullu (2017)**, "A new fuzzy logic based career guidance system: WEB-CGS," *Tehnički vjesnik* 24. DOI 10.17559/TV-20151105201325. Country: Turkey. Inputs: prior academic success and **teacher views**. Algorithm: a fuzzy-logic web service. It predicts **9th-grade** vocational-school students' interest across 4 fields (IT, electrics-electronics, accounting, automotive). Tested on 300 students with "promising results", but metrics are not in the abstract. — [Tehnički vjesnik](https://doi.org/10.17559/TV-20151105201325)
16. **Marvee Cheska B. et al. (2019)**, "A Career Track Recommender System for Senior High School Students using Fuzzy Logic," *IJATCSE* 8(5). DOI 10.30534/ijatcse/2019/97852019. Country: Philippines, choosing a senior-high track (grade 11). Uses feature-selection filtering to pick crisp inputs for a fuzzy model. Reports MAE and RMSE and **nDCG = 0.948**. Flag: IJATCSE was removed from Scopus and is widely regarded as low-quality/predatory. — [IJATCSE](https://doi.org/10.30534/ijatcse/2019/97852019)
17. **Otero Gómez, Correa Manrique, Becerra Sierra, Toro et al. (2020/2021)**, "Career Recommendation System for High-School Seniors based on Government Standardized-Tests Results," OSF Preprint. DOI 10.31219/osf.io/5xwf6. Country: Colombia. Inputs: Saber 11 national test scores plus demographic, socio-economic and family data, linked to how similar students later performed in undergraduate study and on the Saber Pro exam. Algorithms: ML and deep learning, one model per career, with a **mean AUC of 0.86** that varies widely between careers. This is a useful template for linking national exam data (comparable to BISE matric results) to outcomes. It is a preprint and not peer reviewed. — [OSF](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/5xwf6)
18. **Ibrahim, Yang, Ndzi, Yang, Al-Maliki (2018)**, "Ontology-Based Personalized Course Recommendation Framework," *IEEE Access* 7. DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2018.2889635. Country: UK. OPCR is a hybrid of CF and content-based filtering that uses hierarchical ontology similarity and maps course profiles to job profiles for prospective students. 138 citations. — [IEEE Access](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2018.2889635)
19. **Siswipraptini, Warnars, Ramadhan, Budiharto (2024)**, "Personalized Career-Path Recommendation Model for IT Students in Indonesia," *IEEE Access* 12. DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3381032. Uses personalised Naive Bayes over job profiles, **personality types** and subjects, with 104 students. Validated through focus groups with IT professionals and **3 psychologists**, and over 83% of users were satisfied. This expert-validation pattern is applicable where outcome data is lacking. — [IEEE Access](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3381032)
20. **Song, Shin, Tang, Hanna, Behrend (2022)**, "Investigating machine learning's capacity to enhance the prediction of career choices," *Personnel Psychology*. DOI 10.1111/peps.12529. Country: USA, with **N = 81,267**. It compared traditional **RIASEC interest-profile matching** against an ML-augmented method for predicting occupation and aspirations. **ML was more accurate overall, but it over-predicted high-base-rate job categories and under-predicted rare ones.** This is a key caution: accuracy-optimised ML pushes users toward majority options. It is the highest-quality psychometrics-plus-ML study found. — [Personnel Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12529)
21. **Rahim & Basheer (2025)**, "A Hierarchical and Multi-Tiered Personalized Career Recommender System Tailored to Individual Aptitudes," *Indian Journal of Science and Technology* 18(3). DOI 10.17485/IJST/v18i3.3979. Country: India. A content-based recommender over **612 careers whose ability profiles were extracted from O\*NET**, designed explicitly for **cold start and data sparsity**. It visualises the recommended clusters against the user profile as a form of explanation. It reports accuracy, precision, recall and F1 all "exceeding 99%", which is implausible for a real recommendation task and probably reflects synthetic or self-labelled evaluation. Flag: low-tier venue. The O\*NET ability-profile design is still reusable. — [IJST](https://doi.org/10.17485/IJST/v18i3.3979)

**D. Pakistan-specific (non-algorithmic, but relevant for features and validation)**

- **Saya, Aslam, Habib (2025)**, "Career Decision Confidence in Pakistani Youth: An Evaluation of General and Psychometric Counseling," *Psychology in the Schools* (Wiley). DOI 10.1002/pits.23552. Validated the Career Decision Self-Efficacy Scale with **1,445 Pakistani students**. Students counselled with **psychometric testing showed higher career-decision confidence** than those who received general counselling. This is a usable outcome measure for an app pilot. — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1002/pits.23552)
- **Zeb, Ali, Hussain (2025)**, "Factors Associated with Subjects' Selection Among Secondary School Students in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan," *Global Social Sciences Review*. The same team published on career-guidance facilities in KP (DOI 10.47205/jdss.2025(6-iii)14). — [GSSR](https://doi.org/10.31703/gssr.2025(x-iii).03)
- **Nawaz, Hamid, Shabbir, Blfgeh, Saleem (2026)**, "SHELF: A Gender-Sensitive Hybrid AI Framework for Advancing Educational Equity in Underserved Regions," *JITE: Innovations in Practice*. DOI 10.28945/5817. Pakistani authors. A conceptual framework based on a 50-study systematic review (2015-2025). It combines gender-sensitive profiling, bias-conscious recommendation and low-bandwidth adaptability. It covers learning resources, not streams, and is conceptual only with no empirical evaluation. — [JITE](https://doi.org/10.28945/5817)

### Inferences
- Across these papers, the typical school-stream paper trains a classifier to predict the stream a student *chose* or *succeeded in*, from prior marks and SES. For Pakistan this would encode the existing prestige of the pre-medical and pre-engineering groups and existing gender patterns. Makond (2022) lists gender and parental occupation among the top predictors.
- Reported accuracies of 72-87% for school stream prediction come from single-site samples of 300-800 students with no external validation. They should not be treated as benchmarks.
- The design most transferable to a Pakistani app is a knowledge-based hybrid, ranked as best by Lahoud et al. 2022, that produces a top-N ranked list with linked careers (as in the Sri Lanka preprint), possibly adding CF once usage data accumulates.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed ML or recommender paper on Pakistani FSc, ICS, I.Com or FA group selection was found in OpenAlex. Queries on "intermediate group selection Pakistan", "matric subject selection recommendation" and "pre-medical pre-engineering prediction" returned nothing relevant. Pakistani BS/FYP theses may exist on Google Scholar or ResearchGate, but I did not search them because of the tool-call budget.
- Full-text details (sample sizes, confusion matrices, feature importances) were unavailable for most papers. The Mahmoud et al. 2025 systematic review, Sabalza Mejia 2021 and Vu 2020 had no abstracts.

## Q2. Which approaches work with little or no outcome data (cold start), and which need large labelled datasets?

### Takeaway
Rule-based and fuzzy expert systems, AHP and other multi-criteria methods, ontology/knowledge-based recommenders, and RIASEC or O\*NET profile matching need no historical outcome labels. That is why they dominate small-context deployments. Supervised classifiers and collaborative filtering need hundreds to tens of thousands of labelled records, and their target label (stream chosen) is a poor proxy for fit.

### Cited Findings
- A systematic review of **25 career-guidance expert-system papers** found researchers favour **rule-based** systems "owing to their simplicity and broad applicability". It concluded that the relative merits of rule-based, case-based and fuzzy ES depend on the application, and that ES can extend guidance to remote students. — [Gunwant 2022, Journal of Learning for Development](https://doi.org/10.56059/jl4d.v9i3.648)
- Knowledge-based hybrids (ontology plus case-based reasoning plus user-based CF) outperformed pure CF and demographic recommenders for Lebanese high-school students, with 95% usefulness and 92.5% satisfaction. Knowledge-based components address the CF cold-start problem. — [Lahoud et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-022-11541-3)
- AHP combined with an interest inventory needed no training data. It reached 77% agreement with students' current arts majors in the Philippines. — [Lusdoc & Namoco 2019](https://doi.org/10.18844/ijire.v6i1.4118). Many Indonesian AHP and AHP-TOPSIS major-selection decision-support papers exist in local journals (for example DOI 10.37012/jtik.v11i1.2594 and DOI 10.47065/bits.v6i1.5256), but they are generally low-tier and descriptive. — [OpenAlex AHP search](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=AHP%20major%20selection%20students)
- Fuzzy systems have been deployed with small or no outcome samples: 15 users in Alghamdi 2019, 300 ninth-graders in WEB-CGS 2017, and personality mappings from 1,250 students in PCRS 2020. — [Alghamdi 2019](https://doi.org/10.5220/0008071803170324); [Peker 2017](https://doi.org/10.17559/TV-20151105201325); [Qamhieh 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3040338)
- An O\*NET-profile content-based recommender over 612 careers was designed explicitly to address "data sparsity and cold-start limitations" in India. — [Rahim & Basheer 2025](https://doi.org/10.17485/IJST/v18i3.3979)
- Methods that need large data: the college-major recommender used nine years of institutional records — [Stein et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3383313.3418488). RIASEC-plus-ML used 81,267 people — [Song et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12529). Saber 11 to Saber Pro linkage used national test data — [Otero Gómez et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/5xwf6).
- A 2025 PRISMA review of ML course and career recommenders found heavy reliance on **interpretable/traditional algorithms such as KNN and random forest**, basic feature engineering (mostly normalisation), and limited optimisation and deployment reporting. — [Iorzua et al. 2025, Journal of Computing Theories and Applications](https://doi.org/10.62411/jcta.12603)
- Even with expert grounding, validity can be weak. PCRS's fuzzy personality-to-discipline mapping showed only "slight agreement" with the disciplines of satisfied engineers. — [Qamhieh et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3040338)

### Inferences
- A Pakistani MVP could start with a transparent rule or fuzzy, knowledge-based scorer. It would encode BISE/IBCC eligibility rules, marks thresholds, RIASEC-to-stream weights (possibly AHP-elicited from counsellors) and aptitude. Pilot data could then be collected and used later for supervised calibration, for example predicting FSc Part-I success or satisfaction rather than the choice itself.
- Outcome labels should be success- or satisfaction-based, as in Stein 2020 (above-average performance) or Saya 2025 (decision self-efficacy), not "stream chosen". Choice labels encode parental pressure and prestige bias.

### Gaps
- I found no study comparing a rule/fuzzy system head-to-head with a trained classifier on the same school-stream outcome data.

## Q3. Explainable recommendations and bias/fairness (gender) in educational and career recommenders

### Takeaway
Explainability work in education focuses on university course recommenders, through explanation-type RCTs and surveys. SHAP appears mainly in student-performance and placement prediction papers in low-tier venues. Gender fairness has solid work in career and major CF (Islam et al., WWW 2021) and in learning-opportunity fairness (Marras et al., IJAIED 2021). No school stream-selection paper reports a fairness audit.

### Cited Findings
- **Islam, Keya, Zeng, Pan, Foulds (2021)**, "Debiasing Career Recommendations with Neural Fair Collaborative Filtering," *WWW '21* (ACM). DOI 10.1145/3442381.3449904. Found gender bias in CF recommenders for "career-related sensitive items (e.g. jobs, academic concentrations, or courses of study)". It proposed NFCF (pre-train then fine-tune neural CF with bias correction), which achieved fairer and more accurate **college-major recommendations on a Facebook dataset**. 81 citations. — [ACM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3442381.3449904)
- **Marras, Boratto, Ramos, Fenu (2021)**, "Equality of Learning Opportunity via Individual Fairness in Personalized Recommendations," *International Journal of AI in Education*. DOI 10.1007/s40593-021-00271-1. Formalises educational principles and a fairness metric, uncovers **systematic inequalities** in a large course platform, and proposes a post-processing re-ranker. The authors say they did not evaluate it with live learners. — [IJAIED](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-021-00271-1)
- **Song et al. (2022)**: ML-augmented interest matching under-predicted low-base-rate occupations. This is a structural bias against minority options, which matters for girls choosing ICS or pre-engineering in Pakistan if those choices are rare in the training data. — [Personnel Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12529)
- **Makond (2022)**: student gender was among the six predictors that most strongly drove the stream-decision tree. The paper does not discuss this as a fairness problem. — [TEM Journal](https://doi.org/10.18421/tem113-44)
- **Yu, Pardos, Chau, Brusilovsky (2021)**, "Orienting Students to Course Recommendations Using Three Types of Explanation," *UMAP '21*. DOI 10.1145/3450614.3464483. A **67-student randomised controlled experiment**. Explanations that used keywords from courses the student had previously taken increased serendipity (unexpectedness plus intent to enrol). — [ACM](https://doi.org/10.1145/3450614.3464483)
- **Ma, Yang, Ren et al. (2024)**, "A Survey on Explainable Course Recommendation Systems," *LNCS* (HCII). DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-60012-8_17. No abstract retrieved. — [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-60012-8_17)
- **Striebel, Myers, Liu (2023)**, "Career-Based Explainable Course Recommendation," *LNCS* (iConference). DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-28032-0_30. Title and metadata only. — [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-28032-0_30)
- **Afzaal et al. (2021)**, *Frontiers in AI*, DOI 10.3389/frai.2021.723447, and **Afzaal et al. (2023)**, *Technology, Knowledge and Learning*, DOI 10.1007/s10758-023-09650-0. Explainable-AI (counterfactual/feature-based) feedback and recommendations for student self-regulation, with 125 and 102 citations. This is the best-cited XAI-in-education line of work, though it addresses learning actions rather than stream choice. — [Frontiers](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2021.723447); [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10758-023-09650-0)
- SHAP-based career and placement papers exist, for example Usita et al. 2026 (DOI 10.9734/ajrcos/2026/v19i8890) and Jindia & Chawla 2024, "Fair and transparent student placement predictions" (DOI 10.29121/shodhkosh.v5.i4.2024.5996). Both appear in low-tier/predatory-flagged venues, and only titles were reviewed. — [OpenAlex SHAP search](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=SHAP%20explainable%20career%20prediction%20students)
- General fairness frameworks are applicable: Deldjoo et al. 2023 (UMUAI, DOI 10.1007/s11257-023-09364-z) and Wang et al. 2022 (ACM TOIS, DOI 10.1145/3547333) are surveys of recommender fairness. — [UMUAI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-023-09364-z); [TOIS](https://doi.org/10.1145/3547333)

### Inferences
- For a Pakistani app, a rule/weighted-score model is intrinsically explainable ("Your Investigative score and maths marks drive the pre-engineering suggestion"). That explainability is a strong argument for it over a black-box classifier, especially for convincing parents.
- A minimum fairness check would be counterfactual gender-swap testing: the same profile with the gender changed should get the same recommendation. That means gender should not be an input feature for fit scoring, even though it is a strong predictor of the choices students actually make.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that applies SHAP or rule explanations to *school stream* recommendation in a reputable venue. I also found no gender-fairness audit of any stream-selection classifier.

## Q4. Recent (2023-2026) LLM-based career and academic guidance, with accuracy or safety evaluation

### Takeaway
LLM career-guidance papers since 2023 are mostly system demos in low-tier venues. The evaluations that do exist mainly document **gender stereotyping** and **poor local-context awareness**. Under a counterfactual design, LLMs steer girls away from STEM, and they show only about 35% awareness of country-specific context. I found no rigorous accuracy evaluation of an LLM recommending school streams.

### Cited Findings
- **Eze, Lunn, Berhane (2026)**, "Evaluating Large Language Models for career guidance: A comparative analysis of computing competency recommendations across ten African countries," *Computers and Education Open* (Elsevier). DOI 10.1016/j.caeo.2026.100425 (arXiv 2510.18902). Six LLMs (ChatGPT-4, DeepSeek-V2, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Llama 3 70B, Mistral 7B) produced 60 responses. **Average contextual awareness of country-specific factors was only 35.4%.** Llama (4.47/5) and DeepSeek (4.25/5) scored above ChatGPT (3.90) and Claude (3.46), and Mistral scored 0.00/4 on context. — [Elsevier](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2026.100425); [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18902v2)
- **Rojas Constaín & Fonseca Correa (2026)**, "Auditing Gender Bias in Large Language Model Career Advice," OSF pre-registration. DOI 10.17605/osf.io/sx7z9. Counterfactual design with 24 synthetic student profiles, each in a female-coded and a male-coded version. In the **GPT-4o-mini pilot, for a balanced profile with no clear academic direction, at least one STEM career was recommended in 93.33% of male-coded responses versus 26.67% of female-coded responses**. The gap was much smaller when the profile had strong academic signals. These are pilot figures, and the confirmatory study is pending. — [OSF](https://doi.org/10.17605/osf.io/sx7z9)
- **Castro & Padilla Castillo (2026)**, "Gender bias in LLMs: analyzing AI responses on STEM career guidance," *Cuadernos.info*. DOI 10.7764/cdi.64.98546. In controlled ChatGPT interactions that varied only the user's gender, the suggested fields did not differ significantly. However, responses to girls included more feminised-profession examples and caregiving traits, and **advised validating choices with family or teachers**. Responses to boys emphasised technical and leadership examples and consulting professionals. — [Cuadernos.info](https://doi.org/10.7764/cdi.64.98546)
- **Ma & Wu (2025; Dai, Ma, Wu 2026 version)**, "LLMs Amplify Gendered Empathy Stereotypes and Influence Major and Career Recommendations," PsyArXiv preprint. DOI 10.31234/osf.io/375tf_v1. Across six leading LLMs, gendered empathy stereotypes were **stronger than in human participants**, were stronger with English prompts than Chinese, and LLMs **recommended high-empathy majors and careers to women and low-empathy ones to men**. — [PsyArXiv](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/375tf_v1)
- **Akiba & Fraboni (2023)**, "AI-Supported Academic Advising: Exploring ChatGPT's Current State and Future Potential toward Student Empowerment," *Education Sciences* (MDPI). DOI 10.3390/educsci13090885. Put FAQs from a US teacher-education programme to free ChatGPT. It produced "surprisingly high-quality answers" in an authoritative but supportive tone. This is a qualitative evaluation only, with institution-specific limitations noted. — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci13090885)
- **Peeters (2025)**, "LLMs as Correctors of Race/Gender Bias: Evaluating Varying Recommendations in Educational and Career Guidance," SSRN. DOI 10.2139/ssrn.5404550. No abstract was available, so the direction of its findings is unknown. — [SSRN](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5404550)
- **"Letters from Future Self" (2025, arXiv 2502.18881)**: LLM agents simulating a participant's future self in a career-exploration exercise, tested in a one-week experiment with young adults. This is an engagement-style use rather than recommendation. — [arXiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18881v2)
- Other 2025-2026 LLM career-guidance systems are mainly demos without rigorous evaluation. Examples are Yashoda et al. 2026, "Reimagining Career Counseling in India: LLMs as Catalysts" (LNNS, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-20612-1_18); Arya et al. 2025, "SkillSet Sherpa" (LNNS, DOI 10.1007/978-981-97-9523-9_23); Abdelhamid & Roberts 2026, hybrid ML plus LLM (LNNS, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-09905-1_2); and Mallick et al. 2026, hybrid ML-LLM to reduce decision uncertainty (Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18271324). Only titles and metadata were reviewed. — [OpenAlex LLM search](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=large%20language%20model%20career%20guidance%20students)
- **Sah, Lian, Xu, Zhang (2026)**, "Fairness Evaluation in LLM-Based Educational Recommender Systems: A Framework for Equitable Learning," IntechOpen chapter. DOI 10.5772/intechopen.1012999. Title only. — [IntechOpen](https://doi.org/10.5772/intechopen.1012999)

### Inferences
- If an LLM is used in a Pakistani app, the evidence supports confining it to *explaining* a deterministic recommendation and answering FAQs grounded in a curated knowledge base (BISE/IBCC rules, local careers). It should not generate the stream recommendation itself. Gender-swap counterfactual tests should run before launch.
- Given the 35% local-context awareness and the gendered advice to "consult family", LLM outputs need localisation checks for Urdu and Pakistan-specific content.

### Gaps
- I found no study evaluating LLM accuracy for **school stream** recommendation against counsellor judgments or outcomes, and none in Urdu or a South Asian language.

## Q5. Systematic reviews of educational and career recommender systems

### Takeaway
Several reviews exist (2022-2026), but most are in conference proceedings or low/mid-tier journals. The most useful are Gunwant 2022 (career-guidance expert systems, 25 papers), Iorzua et al. 2025 (ML course and career recommenders, PRISMA), Lahoud et al. 2022 (an empirical comparison rather than a review), and Wang et al. 2024 (career *interventions* for high-school students, psychology side).

### Cited Findings
- **Gunwant, S. (2022)**, "A Systematic Study of the Literature on Career Guidance Expert Systems for Students: Implications for ODL," *Journal of Learning for Development* 9(3). DOI 10.56059/jl4d.v9i3.648. Covers 25 peer-reviewed ES papers from the previous 10 years (Google Scholar and ScienceDirect). Rule-based systems dominate, and fuzzy and case-based systems are also covered. — [JL4D](https://doi.org/10.56059/jl4d.v9i3.648)
- **Iorzua, Moses, Eke, Agushaka, Kwaghtyo, Theophilus (2025)**, "A Machine Learning Based Approach to Course and Career Recommendation System: A Systematic Literature Review," *Journal of Computing Theories and Applications*. DOI 10.62411/jcta.12603. PRISMA review. Found reliance on KNN and RF, basic feature engineering and under-reported optimisation and deployment. — [JCTA](https://doi.org/10.62411/jcta.12603)
- **Mahmoud, Badouch, Boutaounte, Zioudi, Boulmane (2025)**, "A Systematic Review of Recommender Systems for Student Academic and Career Guidance," *Lecture Notes in Information Systems and Organisation* (Springer). DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-06725-8_14. Morocco. No abstract retrieved. — [Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-06725-8_14)
- **Mavuso, Jere, van Greunen (2023)**, "A Systematic Review of Customised Career Recommender Systems for South Africa," *IEEE ZCICT 2023*. DOI 10.1109/ZCICT59466.2023.10528488. Title only. — [IEEE](https://doi.org/10.1109/ZCICT59466.2023.10528488)
- **Kainyu, Mwadulo, Munialo (2024)**, "Review of literature on Recommender Models for career pathway selection in Competency-Based Education," *African Journal of Science, Technology and Social Sciences*. DOI 10.58506/ajstss.v2i2.200. Kenya's CBE pathway selection, a close analogue of senior-school pathway choice. Desk review. — [AJSTSS](https://doi.org/10.58506/ajstss.v2i2.200)
- **Shah, Pati, Pimplikar (2024)**, "Review of Approaches Towards Building AI Based Career Recommender & Guidance Systems," ScienceOpen preprint. DOI 10.14293/PR2199.000978.v1. Not peer reviewed. — [ScienceOpen](https://doi.org/10.14293/PR2199.000978.v1)
- **Supriyanto, Widiaty, Abdullah, Yustiana (2019)**, "Application expert system career guidance for students," *J. Phys.: Conf. Ser.* 1402. DOI 10.1088/1742-6596/1402/6/066031. A review concluding that expert systems help educational guidance but remain underdeveloped for career guidance, especially in handling uncertainty. 38 citations. — [IOP](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/1402/6/066031)
- **Wang, Li, Wang (2024)**, "A systematic review on career interventions for high school students," *Frontiers in Psychology*. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1461503. 25 studies from 419 records. Frameworks used were career construction, SCCT and CIP. The typical modality was group counselling (8 sessions × 45 min). The authors recommend a 3-tier model of curriculum, group and individual counselling. — [Frontiers](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1461503)
- **Karakuzov & Akhmetova (2026)**, "Artificial Intelligence for Personalized Career Guidance of School Students: Protocol for a Systematic Review," OSF. DOI 10.17605/osf.io/9zm4n. Protocol only. — [OSF](https://doi.org/10.17605/osf.io/9zm4n)
- **Lange, Twumasi, Koutsouleris (2022)**, "Intelligent Job and Career Recommender Systems: A Systematic Review," protocols.io. DOI 10.17504/protocols.io.5qpvor38xv4o/v1. Protocol. — [protocols.io](https://doi.org/10.17504/protocols.io.5qpvor38xv4o/v1)

### Inferences
- No review I found focuses specifically on *secondary-school stream* recommenders. A Pakistani project could legitimately claim a gap here.
- Quality is a recurring problem. Many primary papers in this niche appear in venues such as IJATCSE, IJISRT, IJSREM, IRJMETS, IJRASET and CANA, which are widely considered predatory or low-quality. Their accuracy claims (often over 85%, and up to 99%) should be discounted.

### Gaps
- The full texts of the Mahmoud 2025 and Mavuso 2023 reviews were not accessed, so their taxonomies and counts are unknown.
- Semantic Scholar (HTTP 403) and Google Scholar were not queried, so citation counts are OpenAlex-only, and some Indian and Pakistani grey literature (ResearchGate, university repositories) is likely missed.
